From survey to handover

Rules move each home from one stage to the next. Every case shows who owns it and what happens next, and it cannot move on until its final checks are in place. The amber markers show where homes can stall between teams.

  1. Survey

    First, the survey is booked with the resident and written up. If two sources disagree, the case stops until one is chosen.

    Can stall at the next handoff: Stale evidence

  2. Assessment

    Then the design is prepared and approved. AI can draft evidence from the survey. Technical sign-offs stay with named people.

    Can stall at the next handoff: Unclear owner

  3. Funding

    Next, funding evidence is checked and the application prepared. A finance approver signs off. Nothing is submitted or paid automatically.

    Can stall at the next handoff: Funding mismatch

  4. Resident agreement

    The resident decides which works go ahead. Their answer is recorded, never assumed, and an offer is not a booking.

    Can stall at the next handoff: No resident answer

  5. Contractor

    Once the final checks pass, works are ordered and booked. Contractors see only their own jobs.

    Can stall at the next handoff: Rework

  6. Quality and handover

    Someone who did not do the work inspects it, then accepts it or returns it with a defect and an owner. Handover is arranged with the resident.

  • Evidence, funding, capacity and resident choice must align.
  • More starts are not the same as more accepted homes.
  • A conflicting record should stop one approval, not all useful work.

At any stage, a safety issue goes straight to a person. Retrofit work on that home stops until an authorised person decides to restart it.

7 checks between draft and decision

  1. You are signed in with your work account.
  2. Your role and the case decide what you can ask about.
  3. Only permitted evidence goes in, with its source and version.
  4. One bounded request, with a limit on size and time.
  5. The draft is checked for sources and freshness. A stale or failed draft stops.
  6. A person reviews it and accepts, edits or rejects it, with a reason.
  7. Consent, quality and finance decisions stay separate, and every step is recorded.

Safety concerns skip AI and go straight to people. If AI is unavailable, work carries on with the ordinary case controls.

Who does what

Same home, different view. Each person sees what their role needs, and no more.

Employees

Sign in with your work account. Your role decides what you see.

  • Retrofit Programme Manager

    Sees the whole programme: queues, waiting times, capacity, funding and closed cases. Can send a case back to an earlier stage or reopen it, and sees the reason for extra support.

  • Retrofit Coordinator

    Runs each case day to day: who owns it, what happens next, evidence, visits and the final checks before works are ordered.

  • Resident Liaison Officer

    Keeps the resident's voice in the case: contact preferences, support needs, their answers and callbacks. Records what the resident says and never decides for them.

  • Building Safety Liaison

    Takes damp, mould and hazard referrals. Safety concerns go straight to people. Only this role or a manager can restart stopped retrofit work.

  • Grants and Evidence Officer

    Prepares funding evidence and applications and shows what is missing. A different person approves, and the system never submits or pays.

  • Quality Inspector

    Checks finished work against a checklist, then accepts it or returns it with a defect and an owner. Cannot accept work they did or recorded.

  • Programme Support Officer

    Backs up the programme manager with the same case and funding views. Can approve funding and sign-offs, but never something they prepared themselves.

Contractors

Same sign-in as employees for now. You see only your own jobs.

  • My jobs

    The jobs assigned to your company, each with its address, stage and next visit.

  • One job

    The works planned and the stage, a named contact at Zava North Homes, and whether the resident has confirmed that each measure can go ahead.

  • Work orders

    Your own work orders, each marked "Needs notice" when a resident needs extra warning before a visit.

  • Never shown

    The resident's name, contact details and reason for support. Also finance, other companies' jobs and AI suggestions.

Residents

A separate site with its own sign-in. Read-only today: decisions about works stay with you and the people who speak to you.

  • Your progress

    Where the works have reached at your own home, in plain words.

  • What happens next

    The next step and who to ask. A person can always help, and phone, letters and visits stay available.

  • Planned works and documents

    The works planned for your home and the documents kept about them.

  • Help and callbacks

    How to reach a person. You can try booking a callback; in the demonstration nobody will call.

Less waiting between teams

Homes rarely stall on ambition. They stall at the handoff between one team and the next. Each of these waits has a named answer in the workbench.

Evidence goes stale.
Decisions are tied to the version of the evidence they used. When a source changes, only the reviews that relied on it reopen.
Nobody knows who acts next.
Every case shows an owner and a next action. A handoff counts only when the receiver accepts it.
Funding does not match the works.
Funding evidence is checked before anything is ordered. A gap stops that step, with a named owner, and other useful work carries on.
The resident has not answered.
Each answer is recorded for each measure and never assumed. An offer is not a booking, and the case shows who is waiting on whom.
Work comes back for rework.
Inspectors check against a checklist. A defect goes back to a named owner and is inspected again.

Work never waits for AI. A problem stops one step, not the whole programme.

These are design intentions tested on synthetic data. Whether homes move faster, and how much review effort this takes, is what a pilot would measure.
