## Executive Summary

Research supports a main-route privacy and Responsible AI story. Zava North
Homes is an owner-confirmed not-for-profit customer. The pitch should explain
resident trust, accountable use of resources, personal-data protection and
named human decisions, while leaving fully local sovereignty in the appendix.

UK GDPR does not impose a blanket UK-only storage rule. A UK processing

## What You May Not Know

Microsoft's official Standard/Regional catalog lists gpt-4.1-mini for UK South.
The nano entry in the Global Standard table is not evidence of UK-only
processing. Regional model availability and quota are separate checks.

Current UK GDPR uses Articles 22A-22D rather than the superseded blanket
Article 22 formulation. Meaningful human involvement needs influence before
a decision affects a person. A review button does not prove that influence.
The ICO's refreshed detailed ADM guidance is still marked draft consultation.

The RSH transparency standard was updated on 1 October 2026 and includes
STAIRs. Its duties attach to applicable registered providers, not every
not-for-profit entity. Transparency does not mean publishing residents'
personal records.

## Findings

### Mission and transparency have different evidence bases

The company profile confirms not-for-profit status on 26 September 2026
while leaving corporate and charitable status unknown (C11). Mission and
stewardship belong in the opening. Current RSH expectations for registered
providers support understandable service information, next steps, roles,
decision criteria and appeals, and tenant scrutiny (W18). UK GDPR Article 5
also requires fair, transparent processing and confidentiality (W16).

Evidence state: evidence-backed for those source claims; actual provider
registration, company legal form and regulatory applicability require the
qualified human owner. Confidence comes from owner-confirmed case evidence
and the current official standard, not comparator practice.

### UK GDPR protection is broader than a location label

Articles 5, 6, 9, 25 and 35 cover principles, lawful basis, additional
special-category conditions, protection by design/default and DPIA where
processing is likely high risk (W16). Health information can be
special-category data; no claim is made that every vulnerability field is.
Works consent, monitoring choice and the lawful basis for processing are
separate. Not-for-profit status does not grant a general Article 9 exception.

The ICO transfer guidance permits covered restricted transfers through
adequacy, appropriate safeguards with the data protection test, or applicable
derogations (W17). It therefore contradicts a blanket GDPR-only UK-residency
claim. Proposed UK storage and regional inference reduce transfer exposure;
identity, provider operations, remote access, logs and backups still need
mapping and contractual review. A UK location is not compliance.

Evidence state: evidence-backed as a statement of the checked provisions
and guidance; the specific lawful basis, DPIA and processing/transfer position
remain unapproved in the project's draft DPIA (C13).

### UK regional inference is a target, not a delivered guarantee

Standard and Regional Provisioned process prompts/responses within the
selected Azure geography, potentially across its regions. Global can process
anywhere; EU DataZone is not the UK (W19). The catalog's `data-pivot=standard`,
`Standard/Regional` section, Europe tab lists gpt-4.1-mini/UK South (W20).
The Global Standard table separately lists nano. No matching Standard nano
availability, target-subscription quota, deployment attestation or successor
approval is inferred.

The existing Bicep default remains GlobalStandard and the retained prototype
option is Sweden Central (C14). Existing chat provider-position controls
distinguish constructed references from actual-mode DPO/Legal approval
(C13). A presentation edit must describe the UK South app/data and UK
Standard-model target, not claim a migrated, completely UK-only live system.
The previous cost model's regional mini rate can inform planning, but its
global nano low rate is not a UK-resident-model quotation.

Evidence state: evidence-backed catalog/provider/source findings; actual
UK-only operation is unproved. A new managed-model version requires quality,
price, quota, retirement and provider-terms checks. Updated provider terms
also say modified abuse monitoring for models released on/after 1 November
2026 will cease to be available from a future announced date, with notice
(W22); do not promise that modification as a universal residency solution.

### Responsible AI requires accountable decisions and effective review

Current product policy keeps models on recommendations, drafts and review
flags. People retain works consent, professional sign-off, financial approval,
safety judgement and independent quality acceptance (C12/C15). Source-coded
Quality L4 flags do not accept works. The Responsible AI assessment provides
bounded engineering evidence but no accepted fairness or whole-project
operational assurance.

Article 22A defines solely automated significant decisions by the absence
of meaningful human involvement; Article 22C supplies safeguards for such
decisions. The refreshed ICO draft explains timely influence, discretion
and authority to change a recommendation (W16/W21). The product's
suggestion-only policy is a stronger design boundary, not a claim that the
law bans every automated decision. Reviewers need evidence, authority,
competence and time; challenge, correction, accessible manual service and
tested fairness remain part of the intended service.

Evidence state: evidence-backed for current policy and legislation; effective
human oversight, real resident outcomes and operational RAI acceptance remain
unproved.
