## Research Cycle Log

### Cycle 1

* Posture: focused; one named external paper and affected manuscript passages.
* Inline execution: no independent lane requires delegation.

#### Wave 1: Wider

Initial HTML retrieval identifies World Model, Hotspan, Darwin, review and Ops
as relevant components. Reflection: prioritise objective verifiability,
evidence reuse and the scope of reported outcomes over headline speedups.

#### Wave 2: Deeper

W1 Sections 2.1-2.3 describe compact evidence viewpoints, Hotspan targeting,
agent-steered Darwin search and persistent hypotheses. Section 3.1 states that
World Model updates are reviewed; Section 3.2 reports a small circle-packing
fine-tuning demonstration, not a general learning evaluation. W2 confirms the
title, 26 authors and v1 submission on 2026-09-28.

Reflection: stable objectives and behavioural feedback distinguish this work
from autonomous completion of a changing feature backlog. Neither an additional
agent nor a document archive alone explains the reported gains.

#### Wave 3: Contrarian

W1 Section 4 reports performance gains across 45 OSS systems, with the 13.8%
average applying to the best-performing 20 only. Its production 3x efficiency
estimate concerns application-work PRs per engineer-hour: 43% of team time,
excluding 57% infrastructure/onboarding. Output enters engineering review
queues. Sections 2.2, 2.4 and 3.1 leave general correctness, reviewing/flighting
and trust calibration open. Section 4 does not compare feature-only trajectories
with scheduled post-feature consolidation or measure an unforeseen change.

Reflection: successful objective-guided optimisation challenges any categorical
claim that agents cannot steer or reuse learning. It does not settle the
manuscript's narrower intervention question. The v1 introduction/Section 2.3
associate 22x with OpenEvolve, while Section 4 reports 22x versus MAP-Elites and
17x versus OpenEvolve. Avoid importing this inconsistent headline multiplier.
