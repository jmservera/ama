## Research Brief

* What to research: identity, provenance and relevant claims of the scholarly
  sources suggested in the supplied editorial critique.
* Why it matters: avoid importing mismatched citations or unsupported novelty
  claims into an exploratory case-plus-protocol paper.
* Audience or intended use: paper author and scholarly readers; findings return
  to the active Documentation author workflow.
* Scope: named critique sources and the manuscript's existing references.
* Non-goals: exhaustive literature review, new empirical coding, changing or
  running the private tagged experiment, research-statistic replication.
* Criteria: prefer primary publication or author sources, preserve dates and
  publication status, distinguish metadata from verified substantive claims.
* Requested outputs: source-specific accept/defer findings and citation metadata.
* Output mode: audit; this is not implementation planning.

## Research Cycle Log

### Cycle 1

Active controls: focused source audit; preserve the experiment. Inline execution
avoids a duplicate lane artifact for a small, coupled verification task.

#### Wave 1: Wider

Plan: retrieve the named source pages; map titles to links and candidate
primary records. Retrieved the Microsoft Research review-agent, belief/evidence
and extrinsic-reliability pages, plus the existing RefactorBench arXiv record.
The HITS study redirects to authentication; the unrelated appendix URL could
not supply the claimed RefactorBench evidence.

Reflection: the critique's source identities and numerical claims cannot be
imported wholesale. RefactorBench's primary abstract says 100 tasks, not 180.
CRBugBench has a primary arXiv link worth checking in depth. The older empirical
papers support caution about belief and environment, not a causal claim about
this repository.

#### Wave 2: Deeper

Plan: follow CRBugBench to arXiv for exact version/status and limits, verify
RefactorBench's scope, and look for the critique-named test-gaming and
human-involvement titles on their supplied author/publication surfaces.

Retrieved CRBugBench's arXiv metadata/abstract (HTML extraction failed),
RefactorBench full HTML and the supplied author's publications page. The
profile identifies primary records for Why AI Agents Still Need You and
SWE-Sharp-Bench. A targeted arXiv title search identifies Building to the Test.

Reflection: CRBugBench is a 9 October 2026 preprint, not an established
peer-reviewed result. RefactorBench Section 3 states 100 tasks across nine
Python repositories; its specified refactoring tasks do not isolate scheduled
consolidation during feature accumulation. It also reports temporary-error
and context-flooding failures, warning against assuming that stricter gates
are always helpful. Newly located titles require checking at the primary
record before their results can be used.

#### Wave 3: Contrarian

Plan: test the critique's novelty and publication-status assertions against
the primary records, including mismatched links and existing references.
Challenge whether Building to the Test establishes test modification rather
than evaluator blind spots, whether human-collaboration work supports an
autonomy conclusion, and whether reproducible benchmarks already exist.

Retrieved primary records for all three new titles and Building to the Test's
full HTML. Its tests remain unchanged: the reported gap is between demo
behaviour and the requested reusable library, not evidence that the tests were
weakened. The source's own broad causal language is not adopted. Why AI Agents
Still Need You reports observed collaborations and associations, not a
randomised estimate of an optimal intervention rate. SWE-Sharp-Bench confirms
that reproducible repository benchmarks are an existing contribution.

Reflection: prior work narrows the manuscript's novelty to its traceable case
and specific prospective package comparison. Neither review agents,
refactoring agents, outcome/proxy divergence nor intervention-aware evaluation
is new in itself. Primary records support a short related-work addition, with
preprints labelled and missing full-text checks disclosed.
