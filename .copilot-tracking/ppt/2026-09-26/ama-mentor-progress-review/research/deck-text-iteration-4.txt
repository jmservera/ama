===== slide-002 (main) The case names five agents but not the problem they solve
  [Request] THE REQUEST (ORIGINAL CASE STUDY) / A housing association with 60,000 homes in the North of England / Every home to EPC C by 2030, while meeting Awaab's Law damp and mould timescales / A small team on spreadsheets, far behind plan / Five agents, from L2 recommend to L4 autonomous flags / Promised: double delivery, GBP 40m of grants, lower energy bills
  [Gaps] WHAT THE DOCUMENT DOES NOT TELL US / What kind of organisation it is, and who decides / Current throughput, backlog and data quality / Which rules apply today, which are proposals, and from when / Whether the GBP 40m is awarded or only available / The problem: it names the solution first; each agent gets one "owns end-to-end" line
  [Takeaway] My working question: what business problem would make these agents worth building?
  [Source] Source: docs/case_study_48_original.md (10 Sep 2026). Gaps identified during research, 11 to 25 Sep 2026.
  NOTES (122 words): The brief is a one-page solution request: five agents with autonomy levels, and promises such as doubling delivery and GBP 40 million of grants.

What it does not give is everything an architect needs to justify a design: what
kind of organisation this is, what the current throughput and data look like,
which regulations are in force versus proposed, and whether the grant money is
real. Above all, it names the solution before the problem: each agent is
described by a single "owns end-to-end" line.

So I set myself one working question for the whole research phase: what business
problem would make these agents worth building? Everything that follows is how I
tried to answer it without inventing facts about the company.

  NOTES (161 words): Three things came out of research.

First, the company. The brief does not say what kind of organisation this is. I built a housing association primer and a modelled customer profile from sector medians. On 26 September I confirmed it is a not-for-profit. Its legal form is still unknown, and I have not assumed charitable status.

Second, the numbers. Sector benchmarks suggest about 26 percent of stock is below EPC C, roughly 15,600 homes, about 3,900 accepted completions a year and a gross programme of about GBP 312 million. These are planning estimates, not company facts.

Third, regulation: 16 official documents, so every regulatory claim has a dated local source.

Research also corrected two claims in the brief. The GBP 40 million is an opportunity, not an award, and would cover at most about 5,333 homes at the grant cap. And EPC C by 2030 is final policy, not a consultation, though I could not yet evidence it as commenced law.

They are research instruments, not simulated employees. They label every answer as a case fact, a sector hypothesis or an unknown. That stops an articulate answer from becoming a fake company fact. ===== slide-007 (main) Each request states a value; none needs an end-to-end agent
  [Before] BEFORE / How do we build five agents to double delivery?
  [After] AFTER / What shared records and gates let existing teams deliver more safe, accepted, grant-claimable homes, and where does an agent beat the same workflow?
  [Challenge table] Requested agent | Value the request implies | Real problem found | What to build instead / Stock Prioritisation (L2) | Defensible capital allocation | Fragmented stock, EPC and hazard data; no approved cohort policy | Versioned scoring and eligibility rules; AI reconciles and explains / Assessment (L3) | More homes ready for design | Assessor capacity, fragmented data and rework | Sourced evidence record; AI pre-fills facts and checks completeness / Grant and Funding (L3) | Grant cash without clawback | No evidenced award; reporting is a data problem | Four-state grant ledger first; AI drafts from evidence / Contractor and Resident (L3) | Capacity meets resident readiness | No-access logged as refusal; frontline context lost | Deterministic decision record; AI drafts, routes, suggests slots / Quality Verification (L4) | No payment for defective work | Late detection of systemic defects | Evidence schedule and defect register; AI flags, never clears
  [Takeaway] Verdict of all eight roles: build none of the five as an end-to-end agent. The gap is shared records, not intelligence.
  [Source] Source: agent-request-challenge.md (26 Sep 2026). Roles read the case, addendum and BRD: verdicts are sector-informed hypotheses, not validation.
  NOTES (144 words): This is the turning point. I stopped treating the five agents as a specification and read each "owns end-to-end" line as the business telling us what value it expects. For example, quality verification means never paying for defective work.

I asked all eight role agents, in parallel, what the real problem was, what cheaper alternatives existed and where an agent would fit. The answer was unanimous: build none of the five as an end-to-end agent. Each request bundles deterministic control work, accountable human decisions and a smaller set of tasks where bounded AI helps. The most memorable row is quality: every role said the real risk is that a pack with no flags gets read as proof of quality.

The "after" question is how I phrase it now.

One caveat: the roles read my addendum, so their agreement is a sector-informed hypothesis, not validation.
===== slide-008 (main) Agents for the messy work, rules for decisions we must explain
  [Layer 1 label] Accountable people
  [Layer 1 body] Safety, professional sign-off, resident consent, investment, procurement, grant submission, payment and quality acceptance
  [Layer 2 label] Bounded agents
  [Layer 2 body] Turn free text into sourced values, propose missing values for owners to confirm, reconcile, draft, route messages, raise hazard and quality signals
  [Layer 3 label] Digital foundation
  [Layer 3 body] Deterministic: property master, published scoring weights, eligibility rules, gates and clocks, resident decisions, capacity, grant ledger, defects
  [Ask] THE ASK TO THE BOARD CHANGES / From / Five end-to-end agents, portfolio-wide / To / Agent-led modernisation of the existing model: one locality, existing teams and suppliers / Rules stay deterministic where a decision must be transparent and repeatable / Each agent capability scales only if it beats the same workflow without it (proposed)
  [Takeaway] Agents earn the right to scale only through safe, resident-accepted, evidence-complete handovers
  [Source] Sources: case_study_48_addendum.md (Where Agents Fit; Agentic Architecture Brief); agent-request-challenge.md; BRD v0.5.0 Document Status (owner decision, 26 Sep 2026).
  NOTES (178 words): So if the gap is shared records, not intelligence, why is this agent-led? Because the records do not fill themselves. What rests on a small team today is coordination, chasing, re-keying, reconciliation and evidence assembly, and that unstructured work is where agents help. Later on 26 September, a challenge to my own measurement plan showed the pilot bundled three changes: a new delivery cell, ring-fenced supplier capacity and agents, so it could never tell the Board which one worked. So I reframed it as agent-led modernisation of the existing retrofit model. Whether coordination really is the binding constraint is still a Phase 0 question.

Read the stack from the bottom. The foundation is deterministic, because decisions that residents, the Regulator, funders or the Board must be able to follow should come from rules. Agents sit where the work is unstructured, and people keep every decision with legal, professional or resident authority.

The ask changes: agents added to existing teams in one locality and, as a proposal, each capability scales only if it beats the same workflow without it.
===== slide-009 (main) Two more challenges: how to prove the value and how to explain it
  [Prove] PROVE IT: PILOT MEASUREMENT CHALLENGE / All eight roles backed the intent but rejected my acceleration dashboard: speed is not the benefit, and about 50 handovers in 6 to 9 months cannot prove a 20% gain / Comparison 1: pilot locality with agents against a matched locality without, same event capture from Phase 0 / Comparison 2: each agent capability against the same workflow without it, review effort counted / The dashboard steers; a frozen benefits scorecard decides; a guardrail breach pauses work (RULE-024)
  [Explain] EXPLAIN IT: TRANSPARENCY REVIEW, BRD v0.5.0 / Fixed my own citation: CON-003 cited UK GDPR Article 22, which the local text omits; it now cites Articles 22A to 22C / Transparency gate before live processing (AC-079); per-home reason codes (FR-042) / AI capability register and Board assurance report (FR-043); provenance on every agent output (FR-044) / Gap: three standards or codes and ICO guidance are not held locally (OD-39)
  [Takeaway] Proposed (OD-34): the pilot decides which agent capabilities scale
  [Source] Sources: pilot-measurement-challenge.md (Summary; Owner Decision); BRD v0.5.0 CON-003, RULE-019, RULE-024, AC-079, FR-042 to FR-044, OD-34, OD-39; log.md (26 Sep 2026). Role answers are hypotheses.
  NOTES (165 words): Two challenges asked whether I could prove the value and whether I could explain it. The first came before the reframe and caused it; the second came after.

The first was my own idea: a dashboard showing the pilot accelerates delivery. All eight roles backed the intent and rejected the design. Speed is not the benefit; accepted handovers are. And, as you saw, it bundled three changes. About 50 handovers in six to nine months cannot prove a 20 percent gain. So there are now two comparisons: a pilot locality with agents against a matched locality without, and each agent capability against the same workflow without it.

The second was a transparency review. It found an error of mine: CON-003 cited UK GDPR Article 22, but the local consolidated text omits it and uses Articles 22A to 22D. CON-003 now cites Articles 22A to 22C and maps human review to the four Article 22C(2) safeguards.

Still a proposal: the pilot decides which agent capabilities scale.

===== slide-012 (main) Keeping evidence honest was hard; here is what I would change
  [Challenges head] CHALLENGES
  [Lessons head] WHAT I WOULD DO DIFFERENTLY
  [Challenge 1] No company data / The company is fictional. I modelled tenure, residents and finances from sector medians and labelled every value as an estimate.
  [Challenge 2] Agents that sound certain / Role answers are hypotheses, and the roles share my framing. Divergences stay open: L4 quality flags, L3 digital booking.
  [Challenge 3] Sources that disagree / Interest cover 91% or 87%; fuel poverty corrected from 10.0% to 11.6%; EPC C final policy, not commenced law. Flagged, not resolved.
  [Challenge 4] Start the log on day one / Log entries before 25 Sep had to be reconstructed from Git history.
  [Challenge 5] Canonical IDs from the first draft / The first quality run scored 0% only because of legacy IDs; relabelling cost a full pass.
  [Challenge 6] Run the value challenge earlier, and blind / It came after eight BRD revisions, and the roles could read my addendum. One pass without it would test for priming.
  [Source] Sources: docs/reference/log.md (Conventions; corrections, 25 to 26 Sep 2026); agent-request-challenge.md (Divergences, Limitations); git log.
  NOTES (153 words): The writing was not the hard part. Keeping the evidence honest was.

The company is fictional, so every number about it is modelled and labelled as an estimate. The role agents are persuasive, and they read my own addendum, so they share my framing. Where they disagree, such as L4 autonomy for quality flags, I keep the divergence open. Sources disagree too, for example on interest cover, and I corrected a superseded fuel poverty measure. I flag those rather than silently choose.

If I started again, I would change four things. Start the log on day one; earlier entries had to be rebuilt from Git. Use canonical requirement identifiers from the first draft. When I fix an error, search every derived document for it; the what-if study repeated my Article 22 mistake. And run the value challenge earlier, and once without the addendum, to test whether the roles were primed by my framing.
