## Purpose

This primer records general knowledge about UK housing associations so that
research, role agents and solution proposals start from an understanding of the
customer. Proper North Homes, the 60,000-home North of England association in
[Case Study 48](../../../case_study_48_original.md), is one such organisation.

The primer describes the sector, not Proper North Homes. Sector patterns are
not facts about the company until confirmed. Use it alongside
[Company Profile Research](../company/company-profile-research.md) and
[UK Social Housing Baseline Research](./uk-social-housing-baseline-research.md).

## What a Housing Association Is

According to the source, a housing association has these defining features:

* It is an independent, not-for-profit organisation.
* It provides affordable homes to people who cannot buy or rent on the open
  market, through rent or shared ownership.
* It manages and maintains its homes to keep a good standard of living for
  tenants.
* It supports community development and provides tenant support to vulnerable
  people.
* It pursues a community-focused mission rather than a profit motive.
* It reinvests any surplus in the organisation and the environments it
  manages, rather than distributing profit.
* It is heavily regulated and often receives government support to develop new
  homes and maintain existing ones.

In England, housing associations that are registered with the Regulator of
Social Housing are private registered providers of social housing. The
regulatory duties that apply to Proper North Homes are recorded in
[Company Profile Research](../company/company-profile-research.md) and in the
[reference register](../../regulatory/README.md).

## Tenure Types

The source identifies three main types of home offered by housing
associations:

| Tenure | Source description | Verification note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Social rent | For people on the lowest incomes, often around 50% of market rates and subsidised by government to stay affordable | The 50% figure is the source's approximation. Social rents are set under the regulator's Rent Standard and vary by area and property |
| Affordable rent | Rent capped at 80% of market value including service charges, for people who cannot afford open-market rent but do not meet social rent criteria | Consistent with the 80% inclusive cap in the Rent Standard. Confirm the current standard before use |
| Shared ownership | The buyer purchases a share of the home and pays rent to the association on the share they do not own | Lease terms determine the association's and the shared owner's repair and improvement responsibilities |

## Funding Model

The source describes housing associations as non-profit bodies that often
receive government support for new development and maintenance of existing
homes. Surpluses are reinvested rather than paid out.

The source does not describe borrowing, grant conditions or financial
regulation. Those topics are covered in the grant and financial sections of
[UK Social Housing Baseline Research](./uk-social-housing-baseline-research.md).

## Sector Scale and Context

The source reports these figures for the largest associations:

| Association | Reported scale |
| --- | --- |
| Clarion Housing | 125,000 properties, housing more than 350,000 people |
| L&Q | Around 105,000 homes |
| Peabody | Approximately 108,000 properties |

The source ranks L&Q second and Peabody third, although its own figures place
Peabody above L&Q. Treat the ranking as unverified. At 60,000 homes, Proper
North Homes would be a large association, but smaller than these three.

The source states that government targets include 1.5 million homes, of which
300,000 will be social rent, affordable rent or shared ownership, over five
years. GOV.UK guidance for the Social and Affordable Homes Programme 2026 to
2036 describes a ten-year programme from April 2026 to March 2036 supporting
around 300,000 social and affordable homes, alongside a wider pledge of 1.5
million homes during the current Parliament. Use the official programme
guidance, not the source's five-year framing.

## Implications for Proper North Homes

The following points are internal interpretation and sector-informed
hypotheses. They are not validated facts about Proper North Homes.

* Retrofit investment competes with new supply, repairs, safety and
  regeneration for reinvested surplus and borrowing capacity. This reinforces
  the competing capital priorities described in
  [Company Profile Research](../company/company-profile-research.md).
* Rent controls on social and affordable rent limit the scope to recover
  retrofit cost through rent. Resident benefit is more likely to appear as
  lower energy bills, warmer homes and fewer damp and mould hazards.
* A mixed tenure profile may change the consent, cost responsibility and
  communication route for each home. Shared owners and leaseholders are likely
  to need a different route from assured tenants.
* The tenant-support mission means a meaningful share of residents may be
  vulnerable. Consent, access and communication agents need vulnerability
  safeguards and human escalation by design.
* A not-for-profit, regulated organisation will expect transparent governance,
  auditable decisions and board-level accountability for agent-assisted work.

## Open Questions for Proper North Homes

* What is the tenure mix across the 60,000 homes, including social rent,
  affordable rent, shared ownership, leasehold and supported housing?
* How do lease terms allocate responsibility for retrofit measures in shared
  ownership and leasehold homes?
* What share of residents is recorded as vulnerable, and how complete is that
  data?
* How does the board prioritise surplus and borrowing capacity between new
  supply, existing stock investment and retrofit?

## Evidence Limitations

The primary source is a short consumer-facing article from a property portal
that also promotes shared ownership listings. It does not cover housing
association governance, regulation in detail, finance, stock condition or
retrofit. Figures and targets may change without notice. Refresh this primer
when an official or sector-body source provides a stronger account of the same
topics.
