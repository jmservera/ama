# Social housing retrofit, damp and mould, and AI pricing in England: citable statistics for a 60,000-home landlord (as at 23 October 2026)

## Executive Summary

> **Key finding:** In England, about three in four social homes are already at EPC band C or above under the current rating. About 1.1 million (26%) are below C. Government has now confirmed a Minimum Energy Efficiency Standard (MEES) for social housing: one reformed-EPC metric at band C by 1 April 2030 and a second by 1 April 2039, with a £10,000 spend exemption per metric. A new Decent Homes Standard (DHS) applies from 2035. Awaab's Law phase 1 has applied since 27 October 2025; phase 2 starts on 30 November 2026.
> **Confidence:** HIGH for the English Housing Survey (EHS), MEES, DHS and Awaab's Law figures, which are official statistics or policy documents read word for word. MODERATE for the Azure price, which matched three third-party trackers but could not be read on Microsoft's own page.
> **Action:** Use the tables below as the evidence base. No calculations were done. Scaling to 60,000 homes is left to you. Note the bases (dwellings vs households vs units) and the releases due shortly (27 Oct 2026, 5 Nov 2026).

- **Energy efficiency:** In 2024, 69% of social rented dwellings were band C and 5% were A/B ([S1](https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/chapters-for-english-housing-survey-2024-to-2025-headline-findings-on-housing-quality-and-energy-efficiency/chapter-2-energy-efficiency)). Government states 74% are A–C and 26% (about 1.1 million homes) are below C ([S5](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/69ccbc3eb5ac47b0874f3fbf/SRS_MEES_final-stage_IA.pdf)).
- **Cost and funding:** The EHS mean modelled cost to bring a dwelling to band C is £5,655 for social rented, £6,082 for housing associations (HAs) and £5,050 for councils ([S1](https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/chapters-for-english-housing-survey-2024-to-2025-headline-findings-on-housing-quality-and-energy-efficiency/chapter-2-energy-efficiency)). The impact assessment (IA) puts the whole-sector cost of reaching EPC C (or the equivalent under the new rating metrics) at about £3.387bn ([S5](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/69ccbc3eb5ac47b0874f3fbf/SRS_MEES_final-stage_IA.pdf)).
- **Warm Homes: Social Housing Fund (WH:SHF) Wave 3:** £1.29bn plus a further £295m for 2026/27. Grant is capped at £7,500 per home, averaged across a bid, with at least 50% co-funding ([S13](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6a4527e2732d8e7ce5f53aa2/WHSHF-wave-3-scheme-guidance-may-26-addendum.pdf)).
- **Damp and mould:** Surveyors recorded damp in 7% of social rented dwellings (6% for HAs). However, 41% of social renting households self-report condensation, damp or mould ([S2](https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/chapters-for-english-housing-survey-2024-to-2025-headline-findings-on-housing-quality-and-energy-efficiency/chapter-1-housing-quality)). The most serious level (HHSRS Category 1 damp and mould) affects 0.4% of HA households ([S3](https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/english-housing-survey-2024-to-2025-perceptions-and-impacts-of-housing-quality/english-housing-survey-2024-to-2025-perceptions-and-impacts-of-housing-quality)).
- **Reporting volumes:** Large landlords' Tenant Satisfaction Measures (TSM) for 2024/25 show 67 stage 1 complaints per 1,000 homes and 18.7 million responsive repairs raised ([S20](https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/tenant-satisfaction-measures-202425/tenant-satisfaction-measures-202425-headline-report)). The Housing Ombudsman found condensation, damp and mould in 34% of the repairs complaints it investigated in 2025-26 ([S18](https://www.housing-ombudsman.org.uk/annual-complaint-review-reports/annual-complaints-review-2025-26/)).
- **Decent Homes:** Under the current standard, 10% of social rented dwellings are non-decent and 5% have a Category 1 hazard ([S2](https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/chapters-for-english-housing-survey-2024-to-2025-headline-findings-on-housing-quality-and-energy-efficiency/chapter-1-housing-quality)). Under the new standard (2023 data), the failure rate would rise to 45% ([S10](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/ehs-briefing-modelling-a-new-decent-homes-standard/ehs-briefing-modelling-a-new-decent-homes-standard)).
- **Azure GPT-4.1 mini (Global Standard):** $0.40 per 1M input tokens and $1.60 per 1M output tokens. This comes from third-party mirrors of the Azure Retail Prices API ([S26](https://www.azurespeed.com/AzureAiModelPricing/Models/openai-gpt-4-1-mini), [S27](https://www.getmaxim.ai/bifrost/llm-cost-calculator/provider/azure/model/gpt-4.1-mini)).

- **Check markers:**
  - **✓✓** = quote verified word for word in the primary source and corroborated by a second source.
  - **✓** = verified word for word in one official or primary source.
  - **⚠** = secondary or third-party source only, dated, or not England.
- **No arithmetic has been done.** Where a per-home figure is not published (for example SHDF Wave 2.1), the raw totals are given.
- **Bases differ:** "dwellings" (EHS physical survey, includes vacant homes), "households" (EHS interview), "units" (RSH returns, includes shared ownership and supported bedspaces) and "LCRA homes" (low cost rental accommodation, used in TSM returns).

## Conclusion

The national picture has two sides. **Energy performance in social housing is already relatively good:** 74% of homes are A–C under the current EPC measure. The remaining gap (about 1.1m homes) is mostly band D, the cheapest band to fix, at a mean of £6,002 across all tenures and £6,082 for an average HA home. This helps explain why the government's whole-sector estimate for reaching C (~£3.387bn, 2.7) is well below the £11.3bn estimated cost of meeting the new DHS in social housing (4.11). **The main policy risk is the change of measure.** MEES compliance will be judged on reformed HEM-based EPCs from 2030. The IA says the number of homes meeting two of the new metrics "is substantially lower". Homes that are band C today may still need work for the 2039 second metric. Each metric has its own £10,000 spend exemption, which government says is designed to accommodate the two compliance dates.

**Damp and mould is the larger operational risk.** Surveyors record damp in 6–7% of social homes, but about four in ten social renting households report condensation, damp or mould. Under Awaab's Law, the gap between reports and confirmed hazards becomes a workload issue: every report has to be triaged, and potential significant hazards investigated within 10 working days. Ombudsman data show the cost of getting this wrong. Damp and mould makes up a third of investigated repairs complaints, over three quarters are upheld, and it attracts the highest total compensation. Phase 2 (30 Nov 2026) widens the scope to excess cold and other hazards. That links the retrofit programme (thermal comfort) directly to the repairs response duty.

The new DHS (from 2035) raises social-sector failure from 10% to 45% on 2023 data, mainly through disrepair. Retrofit, damp and decency programmes are therefore best planned and sized together rather than as separate projects.

## Limitations & Residual Risks

- **Pre-mortem: "six months on, these numbers were wrong. Why?"**
  1. **Data superseded.** RSH stock (27 Oct 2026) and TSM (5 Nov 2026) releases replace two of the series used here.
  2. **Metric change.** HEM-based EPCs (from late 2027) may reclassify homes now counted as band C, so the 74% and 26% figures could shift.
  3. **A national average misapplied to one landlord.** Regional and archetype costs vary widely; for example, the all-tenure mean cost to reach C ranges from £5,147 (North East) to £8,576 (West Midlands).
- **Cost bases:** EHS cost figures are modelled SAP improvement packages. They are not out-turn costs and the price base is not stated in the chapter. SHDF evidence shows out-turn costs above bid estimates.
- **No England-wide damp and mould case-count series exists.** Complaint and Ombudsman data cover only escalated cases. The Scottish case data are a proxy only.
- **Azure prices** could not be read on Microsoft's own page in this session. Confirm in the Azure pricing calculator before quoting externally.
