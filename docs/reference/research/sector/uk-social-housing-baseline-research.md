## Purpose

This research provides provisional England and UK social-housing comparators
for the 60,000-home Proper North Homes association in
[Case Study 48 Addendum](../../../case_study_48_addendum.md). It supports
planning where company baselines are not yet known.

The figures are sector benchmarks, not verified Proper North Homes results.
They must be replaced with property-level, resident, contract and financial
evidence before investment approval.

Research was conducted on 11 September 2026. Official and first-party sources
were preferred. National survey values, landlord medians, legal service
standards and planning assumptions remain distinct.

Every source, direct report, dataset and derived calculation is indexed in the
[UK Social Housing Baseline Source Register](./uk-social-housing-baseline-source-register.md).

## Evidence Categories

| Category | Use |
| --- | --- |
| Measured sector benchmark | A published national estimate or a value derived from an official landlord dataset |
| Statutory or control standard | A required service level, not an observed sector average |
| Programme comparator | Evidence from a named funding programme or cohort that may not generalise |
| Planning assumption | A provisional value for scenario modelling where no defensible average exists |

### Provisional Delivery Trajectory

Clearing a provisional 15,600-home below-C cohort over four years would require:

* 3,900 accepted home completions per year
* 325 accepted home completions per month
* 6.5% of the total portfolio completed each year
* Approximately 8,450 measures per year if the historical SHDF aggregate of
  2.17 measures per treated home were applied

The measure ratio is a programme comparator, not a target. Property archetype,
starting rating, condition and approved design determine the actual package.

### Awaab's Law Control Baseline

The applicable Phase 1 timeframes are control standards rather than sector
averages:

* Investigate potential emergency hazards and complete relevant safety work
  within 24 hours.
* Investigate potential significant damp and mould hazards within 10 working
  days.
* Issue the written investigation summary within three working days after the
  investigation concludes.
* Complete necessary significant-hazard safety work within five working days
  after the investigation concludes.
* Start, or take steps to start, preventative works within five working days.
* Where the landlord cannot start preventative work promptly, start as soon as
  practicable and within 12 weeks.

The operational target remains 100% compliance. Reporting must also show the
absolute number of overdue cases, the oldest case and the reason for delay.

Source: [Awaab's Law Phase 1 guidance](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/awaabs-law-guidance-for-social-landlords/awaabs-law-guidance-for-social-landlords-timeframes-for-repairs-in-the-social-rented-sector).

### Provisional Eligible-Cohort Scenario

Applying a GBP 20,000 fully loaded planning assumption to the provisional
15,600-home below-C cohort produces:

| Scenario item | Calculation | Value |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Gross programme cost | 15,600 x GBP 20,000 | GBP 312 million |
| Maximum ordinary grant ceiling | 15,600 x GBP 7,500 | GBP 117 million |
| Provider and other funding before contingency | GBP 312 million minus GBP 117 million | GBP 195 million |
| Ten percent cost sensitivity | GBP 312 million x 10% | GBP 31.2 million |
| Funding need with sensitivity | GBP 195 million plus GBP 31.2 million | GBP 226.2 million |

GBP 20,000 is a planning assumption, not a published average. It allows for
more than the EHS modelled energy measures, but the actual programme requires
archetype designs, enabling works, resident support, quality assurance,
programme overhead, inflation and risk analysis.

The case-study GBP 40 million opportunity would support no more than about
5,333 homes at the GBP 7,500 cap. It remains unsecured until a grant funding
agreement is countersigned and should then move separately through award,
eligible expenditure, claim, receivable, receipt and reconciliation states.

### Quality Warning

The National Audit Office reported major issues in an estimated 98% of external
wall insulation and 29% of internal wall insulation installations assessed
within the ECO4 and Great British Insulation Scheme failure cohort. These values
are not social-housing retrofit averages and must not be used as expected defect
rates. They demonstrate the consequence of weak design, oversight, evidence and
remediation controls.

Source: [NAO energy-efficiency installations under ECO](https://www.nao.org.uk/reports/energy-efficiency-installations-under-the-energy-company-obligation/).

## Planning Assumptions Where No Average Exists

No defensible UK social-housing average was identified for no-access, resident
refusal, missed appointments, active PAS role capacity, end-to-end lead time,
right-first-time technical quality, staffing ratios or subcontracted delivery.

The BRD may use these assumptions for sensitivity analysis only:

| Variable | Provisional assumption | Validation approach |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First-appointment no-access | 10% central; 5% to 15% range | Replace after the first 100 appointments or 8 to 12 weeks |
| Resident refusal or withdrawal for discretionary retrofit | 5% to 10% | Replace after the first 100 offers; keep deferral, withdrawal and refusal separate |
| Landlord or contractor missed appointment | Maximum 2% management target; aspiration below 1% | Measure separately from resident no-access |
| Mobilisation before production installation | 6 to 9 months; 9 to 12 months for new procurement or complex stock | Validate bidder mobilisation and certification plans |
| Survey-ready reserve pipeline | 10% to 20% above the completion target | Reforecast from survey, consent and capacity conversion |
| Technical right-first-time acceptance | At least 90% initial contractual target | Replace with independent inspection and defect data |
| Evidence and lodgement completeness | 100% | Control target, not a market average |
| Independent inspection | 10% to 20% risk-based sample plus all high-risk or novel measures | Escalate sampling after adverse findings |
| Retrofit Coordinator capacity | Test 50 to 100 concurrently live complex homes per FTE | Require a stage-based workload model |
| Resident liaison capacity | Test 150 to 250 live homes per FTE | Reduce for vulnerability, geography and intrusive work |
| Consent-data completeness | At least 95% initial target | Include not-yet-asked and unable-to-contact states |
| Identified accessibility adjustments delivered | 100% or documented exception | Audit service outcome separately from appointment completion |

These values are deliberately conservative starting points. They do not create
an acceptable failure allowance for safety, legal duties, valid consent or
evidence-controlled approvals.

## Recommended Use

Use the benchmarks to create an initial scenario, not to fill unknown fields as
if they were measured company data. Every dashboard and business-case figure
should display its evidence status and replacement date.

The first programme discovery stage should establish:

* Address-level EPC and condition data
* Current accepted completion throughput
* Damp, mould, repair and complaint volumes
* Consent, no-access and missed-appointment funnels
* PAS role and contractor capacity
* Actual grant agreement and eligible cohort
* Provider-specific liquidity, covenants and board limits
* Defect, remediation and resident-outcome baselines
