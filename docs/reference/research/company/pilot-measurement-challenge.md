## Purpose and Method

The project owner proposed the following on 2026-09-26. The main objective is
to accelerate the retrofit programme, so a dashboard is needed to measure the
benefits. That needs historical records to compare with. If history is not
available, data recording could start at the beginning of the project. During
the first pilot, the pilot would then be compared with this short history and
with retrofit work outside the pilot.

On 2026-09-26 the eight `company-*` role agents challenged the proposal in
parallel. Each role was asked:

* Where the proposal is weak
* Which measures the role needs to judge improvement
* Whether a historical baseline plausibly exists
* What must be recorded from day one
* Which metrics would mislead
* Which comparison design the role would accept

The role agents are research instruments, not employees with company
knowledge. Their answers are sector-informed hypotheses unless they restate
the [original Case Study 48](../../../case_study_48_original.md), the
[Case Study 48 Addendum](../../../case_study_48_addendum.md) or the
[Agentic Retrofit Programme BRD](../../../brds/agentic-retrofit-programme-brd.md).
Nothing on this page is a company fact. The BRD identifiers cited below were
spot-checked against the local BRD on 2026-09-26.

| Role agent | Main lens |
| --- | --- |
| Executive Director of Assets | Tranche and scale decisions, attribution to agents or operating model |
| Data and Performance Lead | Data sources, comparison design, statistics, dashboard versus evaluation |
| Retrofit Programme Manager | Lifecycle flow, stage timing, capacity and queues |
| Head of Finance, Capital and Grants | Cost, cash, grant timing, attribution and cashability |
| Head of Procurement and Contract Management | Supplier capacity, displacement, contract data and gaming |
| Head of Resident Engagement and Insight | Resident harm, survivorship, equity, privacy and survey burden |
| Head of Building Safety and Compliance | Guardrails, rare events, lagging harm and statutory timers |
| Neighbourhood Housing Officer | Invisible frontline effort, recording burden and displacement |

## Summary

All eight roles accepted the intent. The programme needs a baseline and a
comparison before it claims a benefit, and recording from day one is
necessary because history will not supply most stage timings or effort.
The BRD already asks for this: RULE-019 requires an approved pilot protocol
and benefits register against a credible non-agentic counterfactual before
shadow testing, and OD-18 holds the baseline and target ratification.

All eight roles rejected the proposal as stated, for five reasons:

1. The objective is framed as acceleration. The BRD endpoint is accepted
   handover (BG-002), and no objective is met because activity or volume
   increased. A speed dashboard rewards starts, pressures safety holds and
   resident decisions, and hides queues that move downstream.
2. The comparison mixes three interventions. The pilot adds a dedicated
   delivery cell, ring-fenced supplier and assurance capacity, and the agents
   at the same time. Pilot against "everything else" measures the bundle and
   cannot tell the Board whether to scale the operating model or buy the
   agent technology (BG-012, RULE-016).
3. Pilot and comparison would not be measured the same way. If only the pilot
   gets new event capture, the comparison looks worse because of how it was
   recorded.
4. The pilot is designed to succeed. One locality, understood archetypes and
   protected capacity select easier homes. Shared staff and suppliers move
   effort towards the pilot, which inflates the gap twice.
5. The numbers are small and the window is short. About 50 accepted handovers
   in 6 to 9 months cannot prove a 20% cycle-time gain, safety or seasonal
   outcomes, and averaging only finished cases flatters the result.

A dashboard is an instrument, not the evaluation. The panel proposes two
instruments, a two-layer comparison design, a frozen protocol, a
consolidated scorecard and a set of stop-trigger guardrails, described below.

## Owner Decision, 2026-09-26

After this role-panel challenge, the owner decided that the sales proposition
is agent-led modernisation of the existing retrofit model, not a replacement
operating model. The live pilot uses existing teams and suppliers in one
locality with agents added. A dedicated delivery cell and ring-fenced supplier
or assurance capacity are no longer proposed pilot features, although
assurance needed for safety remains a control.

The former Layer A maps to Comparison 1: existing teams with agents in the
pilot locality versus matched existing teams without agents in another
locality, with identical Phase 0 event capture, a frozen cohort,
intention-to-treat analysis and difference-in-differences against the
instrumented pre-period. The former Layer B maps to Comparison 2:
per-capability task-level evidence through paired shadow-mode comparison and
lawful task-level allocation where safe. Shared staff or suppliers across
localities remain logged because they can contaminate attribution. OD-34 is
proposed as resolved by the owner, pending Sponsor, Executive Director of
Assets and Finance confirmation: the pilot gate decides which agent
capabilities scale.

## Challenge to the Proposal

| Proposal element | What holds | What fails | Panel response |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| "Accelerate the programme" | Delivery pace matters to the EPC C trajectory | Speed is not the benefit. Earlier spend raises peak cash and needs Board approval under RULE-011. Faster early stages can move the queue downstream | Measure accepted handovers and whole-chain flow. Treat speed as a means, reported with cost, quality and guardrails |
| "A dashboard to measure benefits" | Live visibility is needed to run the pilot | A dashboard seen by the measured team invites gaming. Changing definitions cannot support a gate decision | Separate an operational dashboard from a frozen, assured benefits scorecard |
| "Compare with historical records" | Phase 0 already proposes a twelve-month reconstruction | Spreadsheets overwrite status, so history holds some milestones but not waits, handoffs, pauses or effort. Reconstruction favours finished cases | Use the reconstruction as context with coverage flags, not as the counterfactual |
| "Otherwise record from the start" | Prospective capture is the only reliable source of stage clocks and effort | A short pre-period differs in season, grant deadlines and access. Recording adds burden and can change behaviour | Switch on the same event capture in pilot and comparison areas in Phase 0, and count the recording cost |
| "Compare with non-pilot work" | A concurrent comparison controls for season and market conditions | The pilot has extra capacity and easier homes, and shares staff and suppliers with the comparison | Match the comparison, use difference-in-differences, log shared resources and report displacement |
| "Measure during the first pilot" | Some leading indicators appear within months | Late entrants will not reach handover. Seasonal comfort, damp and ventilation outcomes appear after the window | Use intention-to-treat and time-to-event reporting, and mark seasonal evidence as pending (FR-032) |

## The Decision the Pilot Must Inform

Four roles asked the same question independently. Is the pilot meant to show
that the pilot operating model beats business as usual, or that the agents
add value when resourcing is the same? The two questions need different
comparisons.

* Without a comparison that holds resourcing constant, the Executive Director
  of Assets would approve scaling only the operating model, not buying the
  agents.
* The Head of Finance, Capital and Grants would put technology benefits in
  the investment case only if they are separated from the cell and the
  ring-fenced capacity.
* The Data and Performance Lead noted that the answer decides whether
  task-level randomisation in Layer B is mandatory.

## Proposed Measurement Design

### Layer A: Operating-Model Bundle

This layer answers whether the pilot operating model, with its cell, capacity
and tools, delivers more accepted outcomes than business as usual.

* Freeze the pilot cohort before any resident contact and analyse every home
  that entered, including declined, paused, unreachable, excluded and
  reopened homes. This is intention-to-treat.
* Run a concurrent comparison cohort matched on archetype, measure package,
  supplier where possible, building type, tenure and resident journey.
* Switch on the same event dictionary and capture in both arms during
  Phase 0. The instrumented pre-period is Phase 0 plus shadow mode.
* Compare the change in each arm against its own pre-period, which is a
  difference-in-differences comparison, not end values alone.
* Report output per unit of committed competent capacity as well as total
  output, so the method's effect is visible separately from extra
  resourcing. Count the ring-fence premium as a pilot input cost.
* Log the time shared assessors, coordinators, officers and supplier crews
  spend in each arm, and report a displacement index for each pilot
  supplier's non-pilot throughput and quality.
* In Phase 3, a staggered rollout with a randomised work-package order would
  give stronger attribution.

### Layer B: Agent Contribution

This layer answers whether the agents add value over the same workflow
without them (BG-012, RULE-016, RULE-018).

* In Phase 1, run agents in shadow mode on the same cases the humans handle
  and compare outputs pairwise.
* In the live pilot, allocate eligible tasks at random between agent-assisted
  and workflow-only handling, where safe and lawful.
* Measure review minutes, drafts accepted without material edit, error
  severity, overrides and missed critical exceptions by arm.

### Freeze Before Measurement

Phase 0 ratification under RULE-019 and OD-18 should fix and version:

* The event dictionary and the accepted-handover definition
* Clock start and stop rules and the pause taxonomy
* Cohort eligibility, entry cut-off and denominators
* Matching variables and primary measures
* The minimum detectable effect from Phase 0 variance, and the thresholds
* Treatment of unfinished cases, exclusions and the analysis code version

Any later change records a version and a reason.

### Analysis Rules

* Use medians and 80th or 90th percentiles for cycle times, not means,
  because the distributions have long tails.
* Treat open cases as censored and use time-to-event methods, with
  withdrawal and technical exclusion as competing outcomes.
* If the minimum detectable effect exceeds the 20% cycle-time target in
  BG-011, report an estimate with an interval instead of a pass or fail.
* Treat safety, resident rights and quality as must-not-worsen tests, not
  as benefits to trade against throughput.
* Zero incidents in about 50 handovers is still consistent with a true rate
  of about 6% by the rule of three. It is not evidence of safety.

### Measuring Effort Without Timesheets

No historical record of coordination or frontline effort is expected to
exist. The panel proposes the same light methods in both arms:

* Random-prompt work sampling or a two-week time study, not continuous
  logging
* Short diary weeks for roles with informal contact, such as housing
  officers
* System logs of contacts, chases and handoffs

Report effort net of agent review and assurance time. Count effort shifted
onto residents, suppliers and housing officers, and count the time spent
recording. Released time is capacity, not cash, until a budget owner confirms
a reduction.

published but cannot veto it.

company facts.

"unknown" as its own category (NFR-008, AC-065). Suppress small cells that
could identify residents.

## Guardrails and Stop Triggers

The Head of Building Safety and Compliance proposed these guardrails. They are
reported as compliance, never as acceleration, and a breach pauses the
affected work regardless of throughput. The override authority, if any, is an
open question.

| Guardrail | Definition | BRD link | Proposed trigger |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Unresolved safety hold | Gate passes with a missing, stale or unresolved hold, divided by all gate passes | FR-001, BG-003 | Any one pauses the work package for review |
| Hazard response | Hazard cases in the pilot locality meeting each Awaab's Law step, split by source, with the awareness-to-referral gap | FR-003, FR-018, BG-004 | Any retrofit-linked breach or unaccepted referral pauses work |
| Defects at handover | Unresolved safety-critical or material defects and reopened cases by severity, divided by accepted handovers | FR-029, FR-030, AC-043 | A safety-critical defect holds the home; a repeated or systemic defect triggers competent review and holds affected further work |
| Post-retrofit damp, mould and ventilation | New cases per retrofitted home over fixed follow-up windows against comparable homes | FR-032 | A confirmed significant hazard triggers competent review; a cluster pauses the archetype |
| Fire-relevant measures | Measures affecting fire-relevant elements with review before instruction and inspection at handover | FR-035, AC-055 | A missing review or fire-stopping defect holds that work |
| Resident authority | Works started without a current measure-specific decision or authorised exception | BG-005 | Any one pauses the work package |

Awaab's Law timers stay outside any acceleration measure. Section 1.4 of the
local [Awaab's Law Phase 1 guidance](../../regulatory/social-housing-regulation/awaabs-law-phase-1-guidance-2025.md),
official non-statutory guidance for requirements in force from 27 October
2025 and retrieved on 2026-09-19, sets fixed periods, such as 24 hours for
emergency hazards and 10 working days to investigate a potential significant
hazard. The regulations govern over the guidance. The hazard and repairs
service owns these clocks, and retrofit work cannot pause or reset them
(FR-002, RULE-003). Phase 2 is planned from
30 November 2026, within a likely pilot window, so hazard scope may change
during measurement. Legal must confirm the scope before the protocol is
frozen.

## Diagnostic Measures

These measures explain the scorecard rather than judge the pilot:

* Displacement index: change in each pilot supplier's non-pilot throughput
  and quality against its pre-pilot level
* Shared-resource split: time assessors, coordinators, officers and crews
  spend in each arm
* Variation rate by cause, such as survey gap, scope change or resident
  request
* Mobilisation lead time from order to first productive day
* Supplier-caused appointment failure
* Evidence rejection and clawback value by reason code

## Vanity and Misleading Metrics

* Starts, works released, installations or "jobs complete" presented as
  throughput
* Average cycle time of completed homes only, or any average that drops
  paused, withdrawn or reopened homes
* Agent actions, drafts, messages or automation rate
* "Hours saved" calculated as task count multiplied by assumed minutes, or
  converted into cash savings
* Consent or sign-up rate as a target, and satisfaction among completed
  installs only
* A headline "grant captured" that adds opportunity, award and cash
* Supplier-declared utilisation, percentage of KPIs rated green, or "zero
  incidents" and "days since last incident" at pilot close
* Homes with no AI flag or sensor alert treated as evidence (RULE-014)

## What Historical Records Can Provide

| Plausible source | Likely useful for | Likely missing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Coordination spreadsheets | Some milestone dates and cohort lists | Event history, because status cells are overwritten |
| Asset system | EPC and survey dates | Handoffs and waits |
| Repairs system | Work-order timestamps, hazard and dependency holds | Link to retrofit stages |
| Housing management and CRM | Contacts and complaints | Retrofit and stage tags |
| Contractor portals and reports | Appointment and installation dates, KPIs, valuations, variations | Acceptance, and consistent definitions across suppliers |
| Grant claim files | Submission, rejection and receipt dates | Property-level cost and timing |
| TrustMark lodgement and EPC register | Late proxy for energy outcome | Accepted handover; address-matching risk |
| Tenant Satisfaction Measures survey | Organisation-level context | Property-level or pilot-level comparison |
| Awaab's Law hazard records since 27 October 2025 | Response-time history in the pilot locality | Link to retrofit measures |

Valuations and variations are the history most likely to be comparable across
suppliers, because they are financial records. No source is likely to hold
coordination or frontline effort.

## Contradictions and Gaps

BRD version 0.3.0 now includes these proposals. RULE-019 sets the frozen
two-layer protocol, with AC-062 and AC-074 to AC-077. RULE-024 and AC-078
set the guardrail pause. OD-34 to OD-38 hold the new open decisions. The
BRD records them as proposals, not validated findings.

* Before 0.3.0, the BRD Current-State Summary said the twelve-month
  reconstruction establishes the counterfactual for the options appraisal
  and pilot. The panel said it should be context only, with the instrumented
  pre-period and the concurrent comparison forming the counterfactual. BRD
  0.3.0 adopts the panel position as a proposal. The benefits and evaluation
  owner must still ratify it under OD-18.
* BG-011 and the addendum state 20% cycle-time and 25% effort targets as
  pass-or-fail thresholds. The panel expects the pilot may be too small to
  detect them and recommends an interval estimate until Phase 0 variance is
  known.
* Recording new resident and staff data for measurement is a separate
  processing purpose. It needs the RULE-022 gates and DPIA, and works consent
  is not data consent (FR-011). Staff-level monitoring may need DPO review.
* New data duties on suppliers mid-contract may be a variation. FR-022
  applies the data schedule only when a contract is let or renewed, so
  Procurement and Legal must check each pilot contract.
* Measurement, parallel running, shadow mode and independent assurance are
  technology costs under FR-025, not free overhead.
