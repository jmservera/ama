## Purpose

This research supports role profiles for Zava North Homes, the 60,000-home
North of England housing association in the initial problem specification. It
provides sector patterns and questions to investigate. It does not establish
undocumented company facts.

Research was conducted on 10 September 2026 using public sources. Sourced facts,
sector-informed hypotheses, and unresolved company details must remain
distinct.

On 25 September 2026, a [Modelled Customer Profile](#modelled-customer-profile)
was added from official sector statistics. Its values are modelled estimates,
not company facts.

On 26 September 2026, the project owner confirmed that Zava North Homes is
a not-for-profit organisation. This is owner-supplied company information,
not a conclusion drawn from the comparator research. Its exact corporate form,
registration, group structure and charitable status remain unknown.

For general knowledge about what a housing association is, its tenure types
and its funding model, see
[Housing Association Primer](../sector/housing-association-primer.md).

## Confirmed Company Information

| Information | Evidence status | Decision implication |

Do not frame distributable profit or shareholder return as an objective |

Not-for-profit status does not mean that funding is unconstrained or that
surplus is unimportant. The organisation still needs sufficient operating
surplus, liquidity, covenant headroom and borrowing capacity to maintain its
homes, meet its duties and invest in its mission. Any surplus is retained for
organisational purposes rather than treated as a distribution to investors.
The allocation of surplus, delegated authorities and board priorities remain
unknown company details.

## Modelled Customer Profile

Every value in this section is a modelled estimate derived from sector
medians or aggregates. None is a company fact. For the role agents, the whole
section maps to the `Sector-informed hypothesis` category. Company details are
not yet confirmed.

The profile was added on 25 September 2026. Sector rules and national rates
behind it are described in the
[Housing Association Primer](../sector/housing-association-primer.md), and its
sources are listed under
[Sector Statistics and Modelled Profile](#sector-statistics-and-modelled-profile).

### Modelled Archetype

Zava North Homes is modelled as a merged northern regional group with
majority stock-transfer heritage: council stock transferred in the 1990s to
2000s, merged into a group within the last decade. It is the only archetype
consistent with 60,000 homes, a North of England footprint, general needs
dominance and fragmented legacy data. See
[Common Housing Association Archetypes](../sector/housing-association-primer.md#common-housing-association-archetypes).

| Option | Disposition | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Merged northern stock-transfer-heritage group | Selected | Matches every case-study fact, has northern medians available and explains fragmented data. Its scale is larger than any real northern-only group |
| Northern arm of a national group | Viable alternative | Real comparators exist at this scale. The profile would shift towards more supported housing and shared ownership, larger group systems and different governance, and conflicts with the standalone framing |
| Recent stock transfer | Rejected | No stock transfer has taken place since 2015 |
| Specialist supported or older persons' provider | Rejected | Inconsistent with a general needs retrofit programme |
| England-average mix | Rejected | Overstates shared ownership (9.3%) and understates social rent |

### Modelled Scale Position

At 60,000 social homes, Zava North Homes would be a top-ten English group by
social units; 10 groups own at least 50,000. It would be larger than any real
group with at least 85% of its stock in the North. The largest such group has
about 40,191 units, and the next largest have about 32,700 to 37,200 (RSH
group-level SDR tool 2025). The most plausible explanation is a recent merger
of two or more stock-transfer associations, which also fits the fragmented
data in the case study. The regulator's Sector Risk Profile 2025 links
fragmented systems to difficulties in mergers.

leasehold separately from social units. The combined data tool is unweighted,
so the northern shares are indicative. A heavily developing northern group
could have shared ownership near the top of the 15.4% range.

### Lease Interests Needing Review

The model gives about 2,940 shared ownership leases and about 1,750 right to
buy or preserved right to buy leasehold interests. Retrofit responsibility and
cost recovery for these homes depend on each lease. The sector rules are in
[Retrofit Responsibility in Shared Ownership and Leasehold Homes](../sector/housing-association-primer.md#retrofit-responsibility-in-shared-ownership-and-leasehold-homes).
Legal review is required before any design or cost-recovery decision, starting
with a lease-by-lease sample review under legal approval.

### Board Prioritisation Hypothesis

This is a sector-informed hypothesis. It applies the sector order in
[How Boards Prioritise Capital](../sector/housing-association-primer.md#how-boards-prioritise-capital)
to the modelled archetype:

* Northern medians show lower gearing and higher interest cover than the
  England median, suggesting more covenant headroom than average.
* Low property values, at about GBP 38,000 to 42,000 per unit, limit how much
  can be borrowed against security.
* A merged group is likely to be harmonising investment standards across
  legacy stock, which competes with retrofit for capacity.

Zava North Homes' board policy is not yet confirmed.

## Common Sector Problems

### Incomplete Property and Compliance Data

The Regulator of Social Housing expects accurate, current, property-level stock
condition evidence. Sector risk reporting identifies incomplete surveys,
fragmented records, and weak evidence of remedial actions as recurring
governance risks.

For Zava North Homes, spreadsheet-led coordination makes the following
questions high priority:

* What percentage of homes has a recent physical stock condition survey?
* Where are EPC, damp and mould, vulnerability, component, and safety records
  held?
* Which system is authoritative when records disagree?
* Can leaders trace a reported hazard through inspection, action, completion,
  and resident communication?

### Competing Capital Priorities

Large providers must balance building safety, Decent Homes, reactive repairs,
planned maintenance, development, and energy-efficiency investment. Sector
financial headroom is constrained, while construction and debt costs have
risen.

The case study does not yet establish the annual capital envelope, match
funding, borrowing constraints, approval thresholds, or how safety work is
prioritised against retrofit.

### Retrofit Volume Versus Quality

Publicly funded retrofit requires controlled assessment, design, installation,
and evidence. PAS 2035 quality controls, resident engagement, limited qualified
supply-chain capacity, and grant deadlines can conflict with delivery targets.

Karbon's published EPC position illustrates the possible scale. Applying its
77% EPC C rate to 60,000 homes would leave approximately 13,800 homes below EPC
C, but this is only a scenario benchmark. Zava North Homes' actual baseline
is unknown. A separate sector-based scenario of about 15,000 to 15,600 homes
below EPC C is recorded in [Modelled Attributes](#modelled-attributes); the
two scenarios use different bases and are not merged.

### Damp, Mould, and Repair Responsiveness

Awaab's Law Phase 1 has been in force since October 2025 for emergency hazards
and damp and mould. Further phases were still future regulatory changes at the
research date. Providers need reliable intake, triage, inspection, escalation,
remediation, evidence, and resident-update workflows.

Onward's decision to bring damp and mould delivery in-house demonstrates one
possible response to contractor dependency. It does not establish the right
model for Zava North Homes.

### Contractor Capacity and Assurance

Providers remain accountable when contractors deliver services. Common issues
include scarce qualified retrofit labour, variable evidence quality,
subcontractor visibility, financial failure, inconsistent resident experience,
and weak performance data across contracts.

The company must determine whether it uses an in-house repairs service,
outsourced contracts, frameworks, local delivery partners, or a mixed model.

### Resident Trust, Consent, and Accessibility

Retrofit access and resident agreement are distinct from data-protection
consent. Residents may decline improvement works, require reasonable
adjustments, distrust contractors, or face disruption that programme plans do
not capture. Vulnerability and health information can be special category data
and requires defined access, lawful basis, retention, and sharing controls.

The problem specification does not yet describe resident demographics,
preferred channels, refusal rates, complaints patterns, accessibility needs, or
how feedback changes programme decisions.

### Cross-Team Handoffs

Recurring information gaps occur between:

* Stock data and asset investment decisions
* Frontline hazard reports and specialist safety teams
* Retrofit schedules and housing officers managing resident relationships
* Contractor evidence and payment or grant claims
* Resident feedback and executive or board decisions
* Planned maintenance and retrofit sequencing

## Regulatory Baseline

The role agents should apply this time-specific baseline:

* The four Regulator of Social Housing consumer standards have applied since
  1 April 2024, with proactive inspections of large registered providers.
* Awaab's Law Phase 1 came into force in October 2025. At the research date,
  Phase 2 guidance had been published, but future implementing requirements
  still needed date and scope verification.
* The Building Safety Act 2022 duties apply to accountable persons for
  higher-risk buildings. The housing association, rather than a role-played
  employee, is normally the legal accountable person.
* The Procurement Act 2023 came into force on 24 February 2025. Whether every
  provision applies to the association requires advice based on its legal and
  operating circumstances.
* Warm Homes: Social Housing Fund Wave 3 delivery runs to September 2028, with
  grant spend required by 31 March 2028. The case study does not establish
  whether Zava North Homes received an allocation.
* The social rented sector minimum energy efficiency standard (SRS MEES)
  government response published in 2026 is the final policy position, not by
  itself evidence of a commenced landlord duty. It requires EPC C or higher
  on one reformed-EPC metric by 1 April 2030 and on a second metric by
  1 April 2039, with a GBP 10,000 per-property spend exemption for each
  metric that lasts 10 years from the compliance date. Source:
  [local snapshot](../../regulatory/energy-efficiency/social-rented-sector-mees-government-response-2026.md),
  `## Summary of final position for SRS MEES`, `### Compliance date and
  metrics` and `### MEES Specific Spend Exemptions`, converted from the GOV.UK
  government response on 19 September 2026. The
  [regulatory register](../../regulatory/README.md) records it as final
  policy with `Medium` change likelihood, checked monthly until
  implementation and then quarterly. Current Decent Homes, safety, and repair
  duties must not be described as future aspirations.
* Profiling residents using health or vulnerability data can involve special
  category data under UK GDPR and may require a Data Protection Impact
  Assessment and additional processing conditions.

Regulatory details must be rechecked when used after 10 September 2026. The
SRS MEES line was rechecked on 25 September 2026 against the local snapshot
only; the official source was not rechecked online.

   performance controls

   complaints, and communication preferences. Modelled resident prevalence
   exists in the [Modelled Customer Profile](#modelled-customer-profile) and
   needs company confirmation; recorded vulnerability data is still unknown.
7. Governance structure, approval thresholds, board reporting, risk ownership,
   and regulatory assurance. A modelled board prioritisation hypothesis exists
   in the [Modelled Customer Profile](#modelled-customer-profile) and needs
   company confirmation. The organisation's not-for-profit status is
   confirmed, but its exact legal form, charitable status, group structure,
   surplus policy and mission outcome framework remain unknown.
8. Systems of record, spreadsheet dependencies, integration points, and data
   ownership
9. Workforce size, specialist competencies, geographic operating model, and
   cross-team handoffs
10. Outcome baselines for delivery, safety, resident experience, energy cost,
    quality, and grant compliance

## Evidence Limitations

Public evidence was stronger for regulatory duties and sector-level problems
than for internal job descriptions or delegated financial authorities. Some
provider reports were published only as PDFs or were inaccessible during
research. Role names, reporting lines, approval limits, and operating models
therefore remain hypotheses until defined for Zava North Homes.

The Modelled Customer Profile added on 25 September 2026 has further limits:

* It uses comparator medians and pooled shares, so the company could differ by
  several percentage points, particularly on shared ownership, housing for
  older people and leaseholder numbers.
* Resident indicators apply national survey rates that include council
  tenants. They are not regional or company data.
* If Zava North Homes is instead the northern arm of a national group, the
  profile would shift towards more supported housing and shared ownership,
  larger group systems and different governance.
* Lease allocation of retrofit responsibility cannot be modelled reliably. A
  lease-by-lease sample review under legal approval is needed.
* The Warm Homes: Social Housing Fund 30% non-social rule was verified only in
  the GMCA copy of the guidance, and the preserved right to buy provisions
  (Housing Act 1985, sections 171A to 171H) have not been checked.
