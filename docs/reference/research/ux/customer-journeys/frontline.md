## Current-state reconstruction

Maps span enquiry -> understanding -> accepted handoff -> follow-up/closure. Gains are sought outcomes; receiver acceptance is a continuity checkpoint, not observed practice [S2; S5].

## 1. Neighbourhood housing officer

Motivation: protect relationships, involve the right service, avoid unauthorized promises [S3, Role and Decision Map].

| Stage: action / touchpoint | Pain / emotion hypothesis; desired gain | Handoff owner / exception |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Receive visit/call enquiry; locate home and history. | Resident repeats story; officer lacks context. Gain: linked, correctable account. | Officer owns intake; record owner resolves identity, never guessed merging. |
| 2. Understand reported conditions, access barriers, desired outcome and safe contact. | An unsafe phone route or changed preference may be missed; resident feels exposed. Gain: current support instructions without retelling sensitive details. | Housing validates contact; safeguarding specialist takes concerns. Suspected immediate danger goes directly to the established emergency/safety route. |
| 3. Explain service boundaries; refer repairs/hazard, programme or complaint issue with source and awareness time. | "Sent" can conceal an unresolved referral; officer feels powerless. Gain: named receiver accepts responsibility and scope. | Sender retains chase until acceptance. Repairs/safety owns triage; programme owns retrofit coordination; rejection returns with reason and owner. |
| 4. Follow appointment and resident-update commitments by call or approved letter. | Provider no-show is blamed on resident; lost time damages trust. Gain: verified outcome, explanation and supported rebooking. | Contract manager investigates provider failure; Housing checks resident account. No-show never increments resident no-access or implies refusal. |
| 5. Check outcome through aftercare contact. | Mould persists after "complete"; resident feels dismissed. Gain: accountable continuing support. | Repairs/quality owns technical disposition; complaint handler owns redress. Officer records contact, not technical acceptance or complaint closure. |

Missing repair links, acceptance evidence and verified preferences displace patch visits with chasing [S3; S6].

## 2. Contact-centre / assisted-intake staff (proxy)

Motivation: enable usable reporting and accurate transfer, not simply end contacts [S2, EXA-004; S5].

| Stage: action / touchpoint | Pain / emotion hypothesis; desired gain | Handoff owner / exception |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Answer enquiry by phone, letter handling or officer-assisted entry; identify communication support. | Denied digital channel, unavailable device or inaccessible portal creates anxiety. Gain: assisted/non-digital service without portal completion as a condition. | Intake supervisor owns channel recovery; Digital investigates failures, not resident eligibility. Actual channels remain unknown. |
| 2. Search contacts; hear account; read back summary. | Repeated distressing details; rushed staff. Gain: accurate account, visible disagreements. | Intake confirms wording; record owner resolves identity. Check representative authority. |
| 3. Clarify reported urgency, safe route, preference and desired service using approved prompts. | Changed preference leaves queued messages unsafe; staff fear missing danger. Gain: verified route and immediate human safety access. | Human triager handles hazard concerns independently of AI. Contact owner checks pending drafts when preferences change. |
| 4. Send scoped referral; check receipt then explicit acceptance. | Queue transfer looks successful but nobody acts. Gain: receiver, chase owner and next-update owner visible. | Repairs, Housing or complaint service explicitly accepts; sender chases unresolved referral and escalates to duty lead if necessary. |
| 5. Confirm next step; follow up. | Acknowledgement mistaken for understanding; repeat intake. Gain: understandable linked update. | Human reviews message; update owner checks delivery failure and safe alternatives. Service owner retains operational issue. |

Test re-keying and repeated access questions; schedule/history visibility is unknown [S3].

## 3. Complaint / redress handler (distinct proxy)

Motivation: fair hearing, accountable remedy, no disguising service failure as resident non-cooperation [S5; S6].

| Stage: action / touchpoint | Pain / emotion hypothesis; desired gain | Handoff owner / exception |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Receive dissatisfaction through any available route; preserve original wording and preferred access. | Resident is bounced back to repairs or required to complain digitally. Gain: accessible acknowledgement and identifiable complaint route. | Designated complaint handler owns recognition under approved policy; officer/intake must not gatekeep or determine merits. |
| 2. Reconstruct contact/appointment/referral timeline and requested remedy. | Repeated story; contradictory accounts. Gain: sourced chronology with disagreement visible. | Handler requests evidence; contract manager checks no-show, Housing checks context. No invented events. |
| 3. Obtain accepted investigation and remedial-service handoffs. | Investigation request is sent but unanswered; frustration deepens. Gain: named investigator and separate action owners. | Complaint lead owns investigation; Repairs/Programme accepts remedy tasks. Hazard reports bypass complaint waiting and reach human triage immediately. |
| 4. Review response/remedy; explain outstanding matters. | Fast response masks unfinished work; resident feels unheard. Gain: reasoned decision, tracked commitments. | Authorized handler approves response/redress; specialists handle budget/legal decisions. AI cannot decide compensation or closure. |
| 5. Verify remedy status and explain further review/redress route. | Unresolved complaint disappears after a letter; distrust persists. Gain: transparent outstanding work and usable escalation. | Complaint lead retains unresolved complaint accountability; service owner retains remedy work. Human closure requires policy evidence, not AI silence or inferred resident satisfaction. |


## Future opportunities: invented asks, bounded help

Asks are invented, not quotations. Start in synthetic shadow testing. No model diagnoses, infers/secures consent, sanctions tenancies or closes complaints. Named humans review every resident communication, check safe routes and control sending [S2; S6].

| Invented ask / concept | Agent task or proactive action; evidence required | Approval / autonomy; error and manual fallback | Measurable net gain; non-AI comparator |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| "Stop making me repeat it": story-once, consent-scoped case passport (unusual). | Draft recipient/purpose-scoped summaries with sources/corrections. Needs identity, processing basis, representative scope, sharing restrictions and current preferences. Sharing permission is not works consent. | Draft-only; Housing/resident check understanding; data owner approves disclosure rules. No blanket consent. Correct errors; use original notes/structured form manually. | Repeat-story contacts and resident/staff minutes minus review/corrections; compare shared log and human read-back. |
| "Who owes me an update?": promise debt tracker (unusual). | Extract candidate promises; propose owner/date, flag contradictions. Needs source promise, acceptance, safe route and capacity; deterministic reminders. | Staff confirm obligations; no invented deadlines or pressure. Ambiguity stays unknown; manual diary/duty-lead review fallback. | Overdue/unowned promises and net chase minutes; compare owned task list with alerts. |
| "Is this message safe?": inaccessible-contact rehearsal (unusual). | Challenge drafts against synthetic unsafe-number, language, screen-reader, denied-digital and changed-preference scenarios; needs approved rules and scoped adjustment references, not diagnoses. | Internal critique; trained human/interpreter reviews wording/route. No sending authority. Manual accessibility/safe-contact checklist on failure. | Unsafe-route escapes, comprehension errors and review/rework time; compare checklist plus human rehearsal. |
| "What happened to my referral?" | Assemble source-linked sent/received/accepted/returned timeline and flag missing acceptance; needs authoritative events and staffed escalation roster. | Raise-only internal task suggestion; sender remains chase owner until accepted. Missing integration is shown as unknown; phone receiving team and log evidence manually. | Acceptance delay and unresolved referrals, net of false-alert handling; compare deterministic ageing report. |
| "The contractor never came; don't mark me absent." | Draft contradictory-evidence comparison and accessible update; needs resident account, confirmed booking, provider event evidence and capacity. | Contract manager decides event code; Housing reviews message. Conflicting evidence stays disputed; manual callback/rebooking, no resident penalty. | Misclassified visits, repeat failures and coordination minutes per kept appointment; compare standardized outcome codes and liaison calls. |


## Validation and decision gates

Compare matched tasks with/without AI, counting resident effort, review, corrections, latency and support/technology cost. Segment channel/adjustment outcomes without exposing small groups. Gains are unproven; freed time requires allocation, not assumed cash savings [S2; S6].

Pause assistance for unsafe disclosure, missed urgent escalation, false acceptance, unsupported consent or overloaded review queues. Human hazard/safeguarding intake continues during failure, out of hours and without AI. Humans decide staffing, capacity and redress authority.

Validation questions, to ask sequentially:

1. After a resident reports mould by phone, which record and named person preserve the original report and accept the safety handoff?
2. When a provider misses a visit and the resident changes safe contact preference, who corrects the outcome and checks pending communications?
3. When a complaint response is issued but the remedy remains unfinished, who retains each obligation and what evidence permits closure?
