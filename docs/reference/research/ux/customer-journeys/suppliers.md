## 1. Procurement/contracts manager: secure accountable delivery

Trigger: programme need or expiring/underperforming contract. Job: secure safe, defensible delivery through independently accepted work and financial closure [S3, Role Map; S4, Coordination/Quality].

| Stage | Actions and touchpoints | Motivation; pain/emotion hypothesis; gain | Ownership, handoff and exception |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Define demand | Gather scope, specification, geography/dates from Programme, Safety and Finance; compare spreadsheets/contracts. | Buy correctly; anxiety about incomplete demand; gain versioned scope and unknowns. | Technical teams own specification; Finance owns budget. Missing scope/safety dependencies return to owners. |
| 2. Establish route and supplier suitability | Review coverage/expiry, market/framework options, competence, subcontractors, financial health and data handling. | Preserve competition/resilience; time pressure; gain evidenced options. | Procurement appraises; authorised humans decide route/award after legal/financial checks. Unsupported coverage/credentials remain unresolved. |
| 3. Mobilise committed capacity | Reconcile supplier offers, crew competence, materials, client-side supervision and QA availability; prepare authorised work-order references and evidence schedules. | Make realistic promises; frustration when offered slots look committed; gain a credible mobilisation plan. | Supplier offers, contract manager accepts commitments, Programme controls release. An offer alone cannot authorise dispatch; lack of independent QA is a constraint. |
| 4. Control delivery exceptions | Compare appointment outcomes, resident reports, variations, stop-work records and supplier evidence in calls, emails and performance meetings. | Resolve issues fairly; tension between throughput and quality; gain an owned corrective-action record. | Contractor reports; competent safety roles own safety decisions; authorised manager handles instructions/variations. Provider no-show is supplier failure, never resident refusal. |
| 5. Reconcile handover and payment | Match scope/version, quantities, independent QA, resident handover, invoice and grant evidence; chase returns and monitor defects. | Close honestly and sustain supplier trust; concern over disputed evidence or cash; gain traceable accepted outcomes. | QA accepts/returns; Finance decides payment; Procurement manages commercial escalation with Legal. Rejected evidence gets reason, owner and resubmission path; no automatic withholding or closure. |

## 2. Contractor scheduler/supervisor: make a workable day

Trigger: authorised package/capacity request. Job: protect crews and resident promises with feasible schedules, not full calendars [S4, Coordination; S5, Provider/Procurement].

| Stage | Actions and touchpoints | Motivation; pain/emotion hypothesis; gain | Ownership, handoff and exception |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Offer capacity | Check skills, subcontractors, stock/travel; offer periods by phone, email, assisted form or spreadsheet. | Deliver reliably; double-booking/portal anxiety; gain offered-versus-committed status. | Supplier owns accuracy; client manager accepts commitment. Unconfirmed subcontractors remain unknown; no assumed API. |
| 2. Assemble appointments | Match accepted capacity with authorised scope, resident preferences, adjustments and dependencies; liaise through resident-service staff and calendars. | Keep promises; frustration at fragmented contact histories; gain mutually workable options. | Resident controls choices; liaison staff confirm contact; supervisor proposes schedule. Silence is not consent; preference conflicts return for human resolution. |
| 3. Prepare and dispatch | Brief installers, verify work-order/design version, materials, access arrangements and current readiness; send minimum-necessary job packs. | Make first visit useful; apprehension about stale plans; gain a practical, understood briefing. | Supervisor owns crew briefing; Programme owns release; contract manager owns instructions. Missing authority, relevant hold or unavailable materials means escalation, not improvisation. |
| 4. Handle disrupted visits | Receive field calls about no connectivity, no access, provider no-show, hidden conditions or variation; contact resident liaison and relevant technical owner. | Recover without blame; stress from competing calls; gain an agreed safe next step. | Supervisor coordinates recovery; worker stops unsafe work; competent humans decide restart. Changed scope requires authorised review; resident updates have a named owner. |
| 5. Return work and corrections | Collect completion/evidence, allocate QA returns, organise agreed remedial visits and provide reconciled records to commercial staff. | Finish fairly and protect cash flow; irritation at opaque rejection; gain specific correction requirements. | Provider submits; independent QA accepts; commercial staff invoice; Finance decides payment. Sent evidence is not accepted evidence, and an installer cannot self-certify client acceptance. |

## 3. Installer/subcontractor field worker: do the right work safely

Trigger: supervisor assignment. Job: work safely, respectfully and evidence actual work. Payment may come through employer/subcontractor, not landlord [S3, Contractor Capacity; S4, Quality].

| Stage | Actions and touchpoints | Motivation; pain/emotion hypothesis; gain | Ownership, handoff and exception |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Understand assignment | Receive briefing, job ID, design/version, materials, checklist and access instructions. | Prepare; jargon/app anxiety; gain plain-language pack. | Supervisor confirms competent scope. Wrong identity/unclear subcontract boundary returns before work; unrelated resident records remain inaccessible. |
| 2. Arrive and confirm | Introduce identity, confirm the authorised task and agreed arrangements, inspect conditions and report discrepancies by phone or approved capture route. | Earn trust; discomfort when resident expectations differ; gain shared understanding. | Resident may pause/decline; worker alerts supervisor/liaison. Provider no-show and resident no-access use separate verified outcomes; no presumed refusal. |
| 3. Install or stop | Follow authorised design; record actual observations, changed conditions and progress; stop and escalate unsafe or out-of-scope work. | Do competent work; pressure to finish despite uncertainty; gain protected escalation. | Worker reports immediately through human safety route, including out-of-hours fallback. Supervisor cannot substitute commercial pressure for competent restart authority; no informal variation approval. |
| 4. Capture evidence | Take permitted photos/readings, note measure/product identifiers and submit through simple forms, assisted phone reporting or secure offline capture where approved. | Prove actual work; frustration with signal loss, gloves or forms; gain an understandable checklist and receipt. | Worker records, supervisor checks completeness, QA reviews independently. Offline status remains unsent until reconciled; missing evidence is never generated or back-dated. |
| 5. Handover and rectify | Explain operation using approved accessible material; submit completion, respond to precise QA returns, attend authorised corrections and send records to employer. | Leave a usable home and be paid fairly; fatigue from repeat visits; gain clear acceptance/aftercare status. | Resident handover, QA acceptance and Finance payment are separate. Subcontractor invoice follows its own unknown terms; rejection cannot silently become an admission or client instruction. |

## Future agent opportunities: imagined, bounded and testable

All rows are **Sector-informed hypothesis**. Proactive internal drafts/flags require approved access/task/cost limits; external messages require human approval. No agent award, variation approval, contract instruction, payment, hold release or quality sign-off.

| User and invented ask | Bounded task/proactive action; evidence | Human authority/autonomy | Failure/fallback; measure; simple non-AI alternative |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Manager: "What prevents this package becoming review-ready?" | Reconcile scope, contract references, commitment evidence and unmet checks; cite source/date/version. | Draft gaps only; manager reviews, Programme retains release. | Conflicts stay unknown; manual review. Measure net review minutes and missed critical gaps; versioned checklist. |
| Scheduler: "Read these capacity offers without booking anything." | Extract role, period and offered quantity from permitted calls/emails; compare with commitment register. | Suggest only; supplier confirms, manager accepts capacity. | Ambiguous speech returns for clarification. Measure transcription corrections and unsupported slots; assisted capacity template. |
| Supervisor: "Rehearse tomorrow before the van leaves." | **Out-of-box readiness rehearsal:** simulate missing material, withdrawn choice and unavailable QA against actual authorised job pack. | Internal what-if draft; supervisor reviews; no dispatch authority. | Stale data visibly blocks recommendations. Measure avoidable abortive visits; pre-dispatch phone huddle. |
| Installer: "Help me describe this evidence in my own words." | **Out-of-box voice evidence concierge:** structure worker notes against approved checklist; retain original capture and uncertain fields. | Worker confirms factual draft; QA alone accepts. | Noise, wrong identifiers or offline failure trigger read-back/manual capture. Measure correction burden and rejected packs; pictorial checklist plus telephone support. |
| Manager: "Explain why this pack was returned." | Map human QA return reasons to supplied evidence and draft correction questions, without inventing work. | Internal draft; manager/supervisor checks; QA retains disposition. | Unsupported interpretation is withheld. Measure resubmission cycles and net review time; standard return codes. |
| Scheduler: "Could these visits be bundled without pressuring anyone?" | Suggest compatible visits using resident preferences, confirmed crews and authorised dependencies. | Human-reviewed options only; residents choose; no added capacity. | Withdrawals/capacity change invalidate suggestions. Measure repeat journeys, disruption and kept appointments; shared scheduling board. |

## Cross-journey tensions and validation

Capacity offers need accessible any-channel intake, including assisted phone/email. Provide keyboard/screen-reader access, plain labelled states/errors and printable packs. Offline capture needs approved security/reconciliation and model-independent human emergency contact. Contractors receive necessary adjustments, never diagnoses [S2, EXA-004/005/011; S5-S7].

Tensions: utilisation/resident choice; starts/independent acceptance; invoice certainty/unresolved evidence; subcontractor visibility/privacy; audit/surveillance. Chase, receiving and resident-update ownership survives rejection/outage. Compare AI against workflow-only tasks including corrections, errors and cost; no invented targets.

Three validation questions, to ask sequentially:

1. Which actual contract and delegation records govern scope changes, supplier instructions and payment?
2. How do scheduler and subcontractor teams confirm capacity and recover from failed visits today?
3. Can field workers demonstrate evidence submission and rejection recovery without connectivity or confident app use?
