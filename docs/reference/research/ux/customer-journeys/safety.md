## Current-state map A: Building Safety/compliance lead

Hypothesized journey: concern -> scope -> ownership -> action -> assurance [S3, S5].

| Stage: trigger, action, touchpoint | Motivation | Friction / emotion hypothesis | Gain sought | Ownership, handoff and exception |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Officer, contractor, complaint or spreadsheet concern; locate original report. | Protect residents before throughput. | Anxiety: buried/after-hours report. | Accountable response visible. | Reporter invokes independent human emergency intake immediately; lead assures escalation, not diagnosis. Unknown rota requires an owned continuity gap. |
| 2. Compare home/building identity, inspection findings, resident account and proposed measure scope through source records. | Understand affected systems and assurance gaps. | Unease: reassuring sensor and concerning resident evidence conflict; building-parent link missing. | Both sources preserved with dates and uncertainty. | Data owner resolves identity; competent safety/technical professionals assess. Possible fire, structural, ventilation or moisture implications remain questions. No latest-wins overwrite or sensor all-clear. |
| 3. Seek specialist acceptance through referral/callback; retain chase. | Prevent dropped responsibility. | Frustration: receipt mistaken for ownership; rejected referrals. | Receiver, acceptance and next action. | Sender chases until acceptance; receiver owns accepted scope. Rejection, absent receiver or unresolved referral goes to human supervisor/escalation, never an AI retry-only queue. |
| 4. Review assessment/action evidence with Repairs, Programme and competent professionals. | Ensure remedy and retrofit dependencies are controlled. | Pressure: constrained capacity or funding versus delivery targets. | Owned actions and scoped restrictions explainable. | Repairs owns delivery; competent professionals judge hazards; authorized safety owner controls hold decisions. Major funding goes to Asset/Finance/board. Lead cannot absorb liability or release work through a dashboard. |
| 5. Trace verified work, exceptions, resident update and assurance record; report unresolved gaps. | Give executives honest evidence of outcomes. | Doubt: contractor completion or resident silence looks like closure. | Verification linked to findings and intelligible aftercare. | Competent authority verifies; case owner handles closure; Housing owns safe communication. Recurrence re-enters human intake; no flags, no reply or "sent" proves neither safety nor understanding. |


## Current-state map B: Repairs/hazard triage and response staff

Hypothesized operational journey: listen -> triage -> response -> intervention -> verification/communication [S3, S5, S6].

| Stage: trigger, action, touchpoint | Motivation | Friction / emotion hypothesis | Gain sought | Ownership, handoff and exception |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Receive phone, letter, officer-assisted or contractor report; capture resident's words and source time. | Be heard once, act without re-telling. | Stress: interpreter needed, shared phone unsafe, location uncertain. | Accessible intake without a portal or model. | Human intake remains available independently. Emergency concern uses existing approved human/emergency procedure immediately; contact officer confirms safe route rather than sending unsolicited messages. |
| 2. Human triager reviews history and evidence in repairs/hazard record. | Establish appropriate human response. | Uncertainty: repeat damp report conflicts with prior "completed" job. | Contradictions visible without lost history. | Competent humans categorize/prioritize under approved policy; authoritative service controls clocks. AI raises possible flags only; model-negative/error never screens reports out or rewrites awareness timestamps. |
| 3. Arrange competent attendance or specialist referral using rota, telephone and case record. | Obtain someone who accepts the job. | Helplessness: unavailable inspector, bounced referral or no callback. | Accepted scope, named receiver and chase owner. | Dispatch/sender retains unresolved responsibility; supervisor owns capacity exception. Out-of-hours/manual route is independent of AI and retrofit tooling; automation success is not acceptance. |
| 4. Coordinate access, investigation and authorized action with resident and provider. | Avoid harm, repeated visits and broken promises. | Frustration: unsafe contact, missed adjustment, contractor no-show. | Safe access and clear update commitments. | Human response owner handles adjustments and alternative contact with Housing. No-access is not refusal; supplier failure is recorded separately. Competent findings trigger safety/programme handoffs, not agent-issued instructions. |
| 5. Collect verification and explain outcome, remaining actions and aftercare through safe channel. | Resolve the concern, not merely finish a job. | Concern: resident still reports symptoms or evidence is incomplete. | Honest status, correction/complaint route and continuity. | Independent competent verification precedes relevant human acceptance; resident-update owner records sent evidence without inferring receipt. Unresolved/recurring concern stays actionable; neither statutory completion nor hold release is an agent decision. |

## Two imaginative concepts beyond classification

**Referral baton with a return envelope:** follow sent, received, accepted, rejected and reassigned evidence. AI proposes cross-document links/questions; deterministic events and human acceptance establish ownership. Broken integration exposes an owned manual chase. Receipt, acceptance, remedy and work authorization remain separate [S6].

**Hazard change-impact rehearsal:** simulate communal/home dependencies before measure revision: what needs professional re-review, who is consulted, what if contact/inspection fails? Tabletop only, not prediction, diagnosis or release recommendation. Compare checklist-led multidisciplinary review; discard if invented dependencies outweigh useful omissions found [S2, S5].


## Three research questions

1. Who accepts emergency/unresolved referrals, including out of hours, and what proves acceptance?
2. Can staff trace one conflicting resident/inspection case through competent action, verification and safe resident communication?
3. Which building-linked design changes require specialist review, and where are incomplete safety records and remediation funding conflicts escalated?
