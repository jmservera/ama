## Use and Precedence

Use [AMA Scientific Paper Writer and Reviewer](../../../.github/agents/ama-scientific-paper-writer-reviewer.agent.md)
for mode, evidence-state and editing boundaries. These techniques are optional
writing aids, not proof of scientific quality or a comprehensive peer-review
ethics standard. The source summaries are original paraphrases.

Actual supplied or verified journal and article-type requirements, including
confidentiality, AI use and disclosure rules, outrank guide preferences, subject
to integrity, evidence and approved study or protocol constraints. Without a
target venue's rules, assistance is generic rather than a compliance assessment.

## Practical Techniques

* Start with the contribution and reader question. Outline how evidence supports
  the argument, including rival explanations and limits, before polishing prose
  (W1 steps 1-9; W3 pp. 41-43).
* Connect the reasons for actual methods to results and interpretations; keep
  observations separate from hypotheses and proposals. Examine unsupported
  connections instead of hiding inconvenient or null findings (W1 steps 3-9
  and table pcbi.1013505.t002).
* Give each paragraph a purpose and a clear transition. Prefer precise subjects,
  verbs and defined terms; shorten without losing uncertainty, controls,
  explanation or attribution (W1 steps 11-15; W3 pp. 6-14; W4 tips 5-8).
* Make captions readable with the figure: define units, sample size, uncertainty,
  parameters and tests where relevant and known. Check agreement among title,
  abstract, figures, conclusions and references (W1 step 12; W2 tips 1-3, 7, 13
  and 19; W3 pp. 30-40).
* Choose a drafting order that helps the author: thesis and reasoning first
  (W1), methods and results first (W2 tip 12), or outline and figures first
  (W3 p. 16). None is a universal sequence.
* Revise for meaning as well as wording. Optional read-aloud, fresh-eye and
  colleague feedback can expose gaps; they do not establish independent peer
  review (W2 tips 10-11 and 20; W4 tips 8-9).

## Genre Choices

Genre routing below is a synthesis, not a format prescribed identically by all
four guides. Empirical-format advice in W3 p. 16 and W4 tip 4 is conditional.

* Empirical reports can use IMRaD when appropriate to the actual study and venue.
  Completed methods and results need real evidence, not template completion.
* Conceptual papers and perspectives need a thesis, reasoning, evidence,
  counterarguments, limits and implications, not fictional methods or results.
* Methods papers can organize around design, assumptions, use and evaluation.
  Distinguish a proposed method from a performed and evidenced evaluation.
* Narrative reviews need an explicit source basis and synthesis limits. The
  label systematic requires an actual search and selection protocol.
* Systematic reviews and protocols follow the actual design and applicable
  reporting requirements. A planned search or analysis remains planned.

## Qualifications and Safeguards

W1's two-part narrative (step 2), positive ending (step 10) and paragraph recipe
(step 15), and W2's novelty and citation-appeal advice (tips 4-5), are optional
preferences. They do not justify overstated novelty, omitted negative findings
or judging quality by journal metrics. W2's response-time claim (tip 20) is
publisher-specific context, not a promise.

Voice, tense and paragraph length depend on meaning, genre and venue. W4 tip 5
qualifies active voice; W3 p. 14 warns against over-condensation. W3's statistical
advice (pp. 17-18 and 28) does not make null-hypothesis tests or a p-value threshold
universal requirements. Describe the actual design, estimates and uncertainty.
Figure processing needs accurate, disclosed, venue-permitted treatment, not
misleading selective changes (W2 tip 7; W3 pp. 39-40).

W3's initial AI recommendations (pp. 45-47) date to January 2023. Together with
W1's emphasis on human intellectual framing, they inform caution, not a current
universal ban or permission. Applicable venue policies and human responsibility
remain decisive.

Evidence-bounded, fair critique, confidentiality, human ownership and explicit
action boundaries are design safeguards synthesized for this repository; the
four guides do not establish a complete ethics code or empirical proof of their
effectiveness. Reviewer-response practice can use W3 pp. 18-19, 56 and 59-64:
connect each actual comment to a supported change or reasoned answer, without
claiming proposed work has been performed.

step 12 for figure definitions, and table pcbi.1013505.t002 for caveat-response
limits. The opening and conclusion qualify the recipe's generality.

[PLOS HTML](https://journals.plos.org/ploscompbiol/article?id=10.1371/journal.pcbi.1013505)
