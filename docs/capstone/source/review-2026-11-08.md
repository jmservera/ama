## Requested Versus Built Autonomy

Use the original brief's meanings, not an invented universal autonomy scale:
L2 recommends; the specified L3 tasks draft with approval or act within
preferences; L4 autonomously raises flags. L4 flags do not mean autonomous
quality acceptance.

The current five registered helpers have a common **L2-equivalent
evidence-proposal boundary**. This is a working behavioural classification,
not a new approved requirement or a claim that every draft must be called L2.
Assessment/Funding's human-review pattern is compatible with the requested
L3 boundary; a preparation checklist is not the complete requested draft.

### Why the Requested Levels Are Reasonable

These are product interpretations of the brief, not new regulatory findings.

| Requested level | Rationale |
| --- | --- |
| Stock L2 | Ranking supports an accountable capital-allocation choice; it does not approve investment or invent the cohort policy |
| Assessment L3 | Draft preparation can be automated while observations, interpretation and coordinator approval remain professional responsibilities |
| Funding L3 | Evidence and wording can be prepared automatically while Finance validates the application and owns submission |
| Coordination L3 | Routine actions can reduce chasing inside verified capacity, consent/preferences and delegations; uncertain or changed arrangements need a person |
| Quality L4 flags | Automatic flagging may reveal problems earlier without certifying, clearing or closing work; missed defects and false-flag workload still need evaluation |

The recommended next decision is to retain that task-specific ladder as the
candidate target unless reviewed evidence and an explicit owner decision
justify a change. First complete Assessment/Funding's review-ready task scope;
then evaluate L3 coordination and L4 raise-only quality under OD-32/OD-31.
Keep optional resident service and direct user-value tests alongside those
steps. The current L2 preparation boundary is a staged implementation choice,
not a permanent rejection of the case's higher levels.

## How the Help Runs and Where People See It

| Mode | What runs | Visible surface | Boundary |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Immediate workflow help | Deterministic rules derive queue order, next actions, clocks and ordinary summaries | Role queue, case workspace and Today's briefing, labelled "Summary by rules" when applicable | Automation of a rule is not a generative agent or a new human delegation |
| Reactive recommendation | A permitted staff request captures sources and enqueues a task; a worker generates the typed proposal | Case-card helper controls and "Assistant proposals" | Source verification, then accept/edit/reject the draft; domain decisions stay separate |
| Proactive recommendation | An approved scan checks bounded pages of open cases for registered missing/conflicting evidence, changed versions or missed commitments and enqueues eligible tasks | The same case cards and "Assistant proposals", with invocation mode, sources, unknowns and history | Default off. Requires finite authority, opt-in, release and current access. Source existence is not proof of live background operation |
| Follow-up conversation | One employee chat with five explicitly selected advisory skills; each turn re-authorises context | "Ask the assistant", retained inline case panel and distinct regulatory panel | Selecting a skill is not invoking its proposal operation. No approval/write/send tool |

The state graph is capture -> pending -> leased -> ready/failed -> verified ->
accepted/edited/rejected, with stale/revoked output withheld. SQL owns
deduplication, leases, fencing, reservations and review. Inference runs outside
domain/database locks. Each attempt has one model call and no tools/subagents;
bounded retries consume the same finite allowance.

The default configuration permits no standing proactive run.
The 14 October observation recorded five real **reactive** proposal paths,
6,884 tokens and SQL-backed content surviving a service restart. It proves
bounded integration/durability, not live proactive authority, semantic quality,
effective review or completion of the original tasks.

Today's briefing and proposal cards distinguish rules, model output and
reviewed drafts. Their references establish provenance, not the truth of a
claim. Chat follow-ups are not accepted-proposal denominators.

## Rules, Decision Models and Generation

| Job | Mechanism and reason | Actual status / limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ranking, arithmetic, known policy, permissions and state transitions | Deterministic rules are reproducible, testable and do not need invented prose | Current source. Ranking policy sign-off and full original cohort scope remain incomplete |
| Clear labelled field | A parser/template is the simplest comparator | Do not spend a model call merely to read an exact EPC field |
| Ambiguous supplied candidate / evidence-support question | Decision 1 scores a closed set or abstains; it cannot invent a value or rationale | Historical unmerged #58 recorded source-bound EPC selection. Its retained specification also describes binary support screening. Complete current scoring integration/adoption remains pending |
| Evidence explanation and draft wording | Generative `Ai.evidence`/retained text helpers interpret permitted context | Current five-helper source uses generative chat completions, not Decision 1 |
| Follow-up questions | Bounded employee generation and eligible resident generated answers; deterministic protected routes remain | Task/audience-specific gates apply; no inferred routing agent, unrestricted memory or authority |

The decision-model idea is constrained semantic selection where open-ended
generation is unnecessary, not a demonstrated cost/latency win. Historical
selection, documented support screening and a complete accepted
request-review-application route are different evidence claims. The scorer
provides no provider rationale; application threshold explanations are not
calibrated accuracy.

Compare the same held-out tasks against rules/manual, templates, generation
and a hybrid where relevant. Count abstention, unsupported claims, critical
errors, all review/correction effort, latency and full cost. Keep the simpler
route when it wins. No new model evaluation or paid experiment occurs here.

## Design-Thinking Decision Trace

These are desk-research hypotheses, owner feedback, prototypes and engineering
observations, not completed resident research or measured gains.

| Challenge / pain hypothesis | Alternative explored | Product decision and intended improvement | Evidence and remaining test |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Richer evidence might permit end-to-end assessment | Geotagged photos/invoices and structured survey inputs | Source/version checklists and preparation, with professional review. Reduce re-keying/rework without fabricated observations | What-if study; helper capture and version tests. Provenance/calibration and qualified task/value acceptance remain |
| An available slot might be enough to coordinate automatically | Contractor APIs and richer capacity/preferences | Separate offer from commitment; require actual capacity and supplied confirmations. Avoid false bookings and blaming provider failure on a resident | Journey D04 and ADR 0003; appointment mechanics. Operational connector/capacity and resident tests remain |
| Residents repeat their story or cannot find the next update | Staff-only chasing versus optional self-service | Purpose-scoped case, named update owner, optional website/chat plus assisted continuity. Improve clarity without mandatory digital work | Atlas D01/D05 and #441 direction; resident source. Compare inclusion, comprehension and total resident/staff effort |
| Changed evidence leaves an old acceptance apparently valid | One overall green status versus independent versioned decisions | Invalidate only dependent technical/QA/handover readiness; preserve distinct remedies and Finance closure | Journey D03/D05, QA/continuity records. Whole-journey and meaningful oversight acceptance remain |
| Dense screens and jargon obscure who acts next | Role picker/trace-heavy prototype versus plain role queue and full case | Clear owner, next action, visible context and "Use this draft"; simplify language, not controls | 7 October owner feedback; 8 October simplicity critique. No representative ease-of-use result |
| A generic model may be unnecessary for structured decisions | Rules/parser, closed-set scorer, generation and hybrid | Fit mechanism to task; use rules first and retain abstention/manual paths | Historical #58 and model-fit specification. Held-out quality, calibration and complete effort/cost comparison remain |
| Quality flags might cause misses or overwhelm reviewers | Checklist-only, L4 flags, recommend-level evidence reading | Keep the current proposal boundary while preserving a testable route to L4 raise-only flags | BRD OD-31. Do not call L4 unsafe or rejected without the specified evidence |

The result of design thinking is a traceable design: owned work, independent
decisions, optional channels and smaller task-specific AI contracts.
Technical tests can show those mechanics; only user/value studies can show
that they actually improve day-to-day work.
