## Page 9 / S08

We challenged the brief to make it shippable.

S08 | We challenged the brief to make it shippable.
TIMING: 70 s for this slide (all builds together) · 07:20-08:30 on the 23:45 narration clock.

SAY
The original brief asked for five autonomous AI agents, from stock prioritisation to quality verification, with autonomy up to level four. We took the brief seriously, and that is exactly why we challenged it.

We ran it past nine role instruments. Their verdict was clear: none of the five should run end to end. So we kept all five responsibilities as five bounded assistants on one proposal service.

We also refused the easy story that the spreadsheets are the problem. As our requirements put it, Zava does not have a spreadsheet problem, it has a delivery control problem that spreadsheets make harder to see. The first investment is data ownership and workflow, then AI.

We kept AI, and changed its job: AI prepares, people decide, because consent, safety and money need a named human.

The portal is optional, not rejected. Issue #441 assesses resident self-service to reduce avoidable status calls and protect staff working time. Phone, email, letter and visits stay available. Portal/workflow comes first; any resident-answer assistant is separately gated, human-reviewed and off for routine automation. No benefit has been measured.

SAY
This is the product. We call it the Workbench, and it is deliberately simple.

Every person opens to their cases. Every case shows one next owner, a plain status and the evidence behind it. When two sources disagree, the case does not disappear into an amber dot: it goes to the person who can resolve it, with the clock visible.

AI sits in the side panel. It prepares a summary, a letter or a funding pack, and a person accepts it or rejects it with a reason. Nothing is approved by a model.

This is not a slideware concept. The work-queue signals, controlled bulk edits, hazard referral, grant and claim states, forecasts and the accept-or-reject AI suggestions are already delivered in the MVP backlog. What you see here is the product view of that delivered work, with fictional homes.

## Page 20 / S17

Seven checkpoints between an AI draft and a real-world decision.

S17 | Seven checkpoints between an AI draft and a real-world decision.
TIMING: 60 s for this slide (all builds together) · 17:40-18:40 on the 23:45 narration clock.

SAY
Here is how we keep AI useful and safe at the same time. Seven checkpoints sit between an AI draft and anything that happens in the real world.

The user is signed in. Their role and case scope are checked. The model only sees permitted evidence, with source and version IDs. The call is bounded, with a finite allowance and an expiry. The output is checked for schema, citations and freshness, and anything stale or broken stops. A person with current permissions accepts it or rejects it with a reason. And the real decision, consent, quality acceptance or a Finance approval, is a separate step with its own audit trail.

If anything fails, the work falls back to its owner, never to an approval. And safety hazards bypass the AI path entirely: they go straight to the people who own them.
