## B01: Why not fix the process and hire more people instead?

Audience: CEO / COO. Return to S02.

### On-slide copy

* Process improvement is the baseline
* AI must beat the same workflow without AI
* Physical delivery capacity still limits throughput

### Answer

We should fix the process regardless. Clear ownership, valid evidence and reliable handoffs are prerequisites, not an AI benefit. The proposal separates workflow improvement from incremental AI value through two comparisons. Additional staff or contractor capacity may still be necessary; the demonstrator does not establish the staffing plan. If rules and templates deliver the better accepted-task result, retain them and report the negative AI finding.

## B04: How do Decision Models reduce cost and response time?

Audience: CFO / CTO. Return to S06.

### On-slide copy

* Bounded candidates may avoid long-form generation
* Compare complete tasks, not endpoint prices
* No calibrated cheaper/faster result claimed

### Answer

Decision 1 has historical narrow real-call feasibility for source-bound candidate selection; it is a learned scorer, not a deterministic decision table. A bounded scoring call may need less generation, but access, serving method, candidate count and human correction determine the result. No calibrated cost or latency win is established. Compare rules/manual, parser/templates, Decision 1 and generation on identical held-out tasks, counting abstentions, errors, review, retries, hosting and usage. Separately, a historical prototype recorded 0.03 ms workflow-only gates versus a 12.5 s median agent drafting step. Those are different outputs, not an equivalent-task speedup: keep explicit policy and arithmetic deterministic, then test whether drafting earns its cost.
