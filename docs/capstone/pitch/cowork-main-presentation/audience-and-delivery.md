## Audience and Outcome

Audience assumption: customer CXOs with mixed business and technical knowledge,
plus the AMA panel. Their exact attendees, preferred communication styles and
existing knowledge are unknown. Treat the styles below as communication
preferences, not labels assigned to real people.

The outcome is agreement on a proposed validation gate and its owners,
not production approval. No customer decision or endorsement is recorded.

## Audience Map

| Audience interest | Primary / secondary style | Must hear | What loses trust | Strongest proof and intended response |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| CEO / sponsor | Sergeant / Evangelist | Outcome, choice, accountability, path to scale | Starting with services; unsupported go-live or benefit promises | Same-home journey and a bounded next decision; confidence without premature commitment |
| CFO | Scientist / Sergeant | Gross cash versus grants, TCA, unit denominator, finite exposure | Tokens presented as the whole bill; capacity sold as cash | Explicit fixture arithmetic, historical spend boundaries and measured gaps; agree the next costed test |
| CIO / CTO | Scientist / Sergeant | Runtime, identity, failure boundaries, alternatives, integration | Five boxes labelled agents without a state/authority model | Progressive diagrams and source-based contracts; accept that simplicity is deliberate |
| COO / asset executive | Sergeant / Scientist | Who owns the blockage; capacity; accepted handover | Dashboard activity without accountable next action | Conflict and owned review in the demo; specify the operational acceptance test |
| Resident services / safety / people | Therapist / Scientist | Choice, non-digital route, understandable decisions, independent hazards | Residents treated as optimisation inputs; persona testimony | Separate consent, offers, remedies and professional review; confirm human validation needs |
| AMA panel | Scientist / mixed | Why this design, alternatives, evidence, learning and risk | A polished story hiding incomplete gates | Traceable HVE process, backlog, trade-offs, Tokenomics and candid unknowns; assess the reasoning, not an invented score |

Deliberate sequence: Sergeant opening -> Therapist same-home context ->
Scientist evidence/architecture/economics -> Evangelist delivery potential ->
Sergeant next decision. Preserve one factual core through all these frames.

## Timing and Interruption Contract

| Block | Slides | Planned narration |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Outcome, constraint, human journey | S01-S03 | 03:30 |
| Operating model, controls, AI and five responsibilities | S04-S07 | 06:00 |
| Value, cost, runtime and Tokenomics | S08-S11 | 06:00 |
| HVE, backlog and learning | S12-S14 | 04:30 |
| Acceptance, next gate, demo introduction | S15-S17 | 03:30 |
| Close after demo | S18 | 00:30 |
| Planned narration | S01-S18 | 24:00 |
| Discussion reserve, inside presentation | Throughout | 06:00 |
| Separate demo, including its own recovery/discussion reserve | After S17 | 10:00 |
| Total meeting budget | All of the above | 40:00 |

The narration clock is a planning tool. The meeting wall clock never stops.
At minute 30 of the combined pre-demo presentation segment, leave time for
the separately budgeted closing 30 seconds: the planned demo should begin by
29:30 if the full discussion reserve has been used.

Allow roughly four 45-second answers plus three minutes for shorter exchanges,
not a new open-ended Q&A after every section. A full backup explanation
replaces detail on the main route; it does not add free time.

## Shorter Routes Without Losing the Argument

If interruptions exceed the reserve, explicitly switch route; do not speed-read.

* Recover about three minutes: compress S12-S14 into a 90-second method
  summary instead of the planned 4:30. Still say design thinking, iterative
  prototypes/MVPs, HVE RPI, BRD/PRD/ADR-derived hierarchical backlog of
  approximately 180 issues and independent review.
* Recover another 90 seconds: give S10 a 30-second runtime explanation and
  S11 a 60-second Tokenomics explanation. Keep the unknown unit cost and
  proposed-versus-existing controls visible.
* Recover another 45 seconds: compress S03 to 30 seconds and let the demo
  carry the same-home detail.
* Never cut the proposed decision, the resident/human authority boundary,
  the honest status, the AI-versus-rules distinction or the cost caveat.

If explicitly asked for a 15-minute pitch later, use a new timed route rather
than claiming this 30-minute route meets the older limit. This request
authorises the longer package, not a silent edit to owner memory.

## Interruption Protocol

1. Restate: "Your question is whether [business concern], rather than simply
   whether the model works."
2. Answer first in one sentence. State the relevant evidence and its limit.
3. Ask for a decision only when appropriate; otherwise park the deeper detail
   with an owner and return to the current main slide.

When opening a backup during an interruption, use its Last Slide Viewed
return action or a caller-specific return, not its fixed topic link. Topic
destinations are reserved for appendix-index entry. In particular, S01 -> B15,
S03 -> B06, S06 -> B05 and S15 -> B15 must resume S01, S03, S06 and S15
respectively. Returning directly to S16, S05 or S09 would skip material.

Useful bridges:

* "The short answer is no: that is a proposed control, not an observed one."
* "There are two questions there: whether the workflow works, and whether AI
  improves it. We test them separately."
* "I do not have a defensible unit price today. Here is the denominator and
  evidence the next gate must produce."
* "We retained the five responsibilities and changed the autonomy."
* "That would change the authority boundary; it needs a separate decision."

Do not say a decision has been made because the audience did not object.
Capture real objections and decisions after delivery, not in advance.

## Delivery at the Moments That Matter

| Moment | Purposeful delivery |
| --- | --- |
| S01 decision | Upright, still posture; direct voice; one open hand for the decision; pause after the outcome. Dress to the customer's boardroom setting, without costume-like role signalling |
| S03 home journey | Move attention toward the audience rather than the screen; warmer expression and voice; avoid acting out a fictional resident or inventing their emotions |
| S04 / S10 diagrams | Stand clear of the projection; trace the main flow once with a small measured gesture. Do not read every component or turn your back to narrate |
| S06 / S07 autonomy | Calm contrast: "rules", "models", "people". Deliberate hand separation, not defensive gestures. Pause after "not five autonomous managers" |
| S09 / S11 cost and unknowns | Slow down for currencies and denominators; keep eye contact when saying "unknown". No apologetic smile or artificial confidence |
| S12 / S13 method | Add energy and forward movement to explain how learning compounded. One concrete change is more persuasive than a tooling inventory |
| Demo failure | Stay physically still; state the limit without blame; switch once. Never narrate frantic repair commands |
| S18 close | Return to the original position and outcome sentence; direct, concise voice; stop after the requested next decision |

## Questions That Need a Human Decision

Before final deck freeze, resolve with the owner/mentor: exact audience,
interpretation of the Tokenomics clue, the unit denominator/quality floor,
whether any fresh measured batch is separately authorised, and the route
that actually passes demo preflight. These are pending decisions, not blockers
to authoring the supplied folder and not permission to invent the answers.
