\section{Testing the harness: a prospective experiment}\label{sec:experiment}

The experiment asks whether scheduled correction improves the repository
inherited by later feature work. RQ3 is addressed by the prospective design
specified below.
The 1 November amendment supersedes v4's two runs per arm/model.
The 2 November revision replaces that amendment's fixed 33-per-cell
allocation with a prospective researcher choice of 12, 21 or 33, default 12.
Historical freezes, diagnostics and the original owner-reported OSF
registration are retained; none approves this new design. The researcher
kit requires an approved source-bound packet and prospective OSF amendment
before collection. Agent configuration
protocols and empirical-study guidance inform this pre-data design
~\cite{lulla2026protocol,baltes2026guidelines,wohlin2012experimentation}.

\subsection{Design and estimands}\label{sec:protocolsummary}

One independent replicate is a fresh repository trajectory through S1's
ten ordered features and a final held-out change request, CR1.
Every selected model, GPT~6.1 Sol, Sonnet~5.5 and Gemini~4 Argon,
runs every arm. Randomised interleaving within model/replicate blocks
uses seed 2027. Feature steps, tests and checkpoints are repeated
observations within a trajectory, not independent replicates; agent
sessions are not independent units either. The selected number $n$ applies
\emph{per model per arm}, equally across all fifteen cells.

\subsection{Outcomes and retained-contract loss}\label{sec:decaymeasurement}

The primary outcome is final behavioural coverage: contracts passed out
of all 127 scheduled feature contracts at $P_{10}$, before CR1.
Coverage combines acquisition, retention and repair.
Retained-contract loss and CR1 adaptation are distinct secondary outcomes,
with legacy identifiers H2 and H6 retained only for data traceability.
HIR/IDR definitions are in the supplementary appendices;
comprehension is not measured.

For trajectory $r$, $F_t$ is the retained state after feature step $t$ and
$P_t$ the state after its assigned post-step; these source states coincide
in A and D. Let $\mathcal{R}_{r,t}$ contain eligible, unchanged contracts
that passed at least one earlier end-of-cycle checkpoint $P_u$, $u<t$.
Superseded contracts leave this risk set. At $F_t$ and $P_t$, let
$\mathcal{O}^{p}_{r,t}\subseteq\mathcal{R}_{r,t}$ contain contracts with
observed binary results $S^p_{r,t}(q)$, $p\in\{F,P\}$. Report
\begin{equation}
L^p_{r,t}=\sum_{q\in\mathcal{O}^{p}_{r,t}}
\mathbf{1}\{S^p_{r,t}(q)=0\},\qquad
d^p_{r,t}=\frac{L^p_{r,t}}{|\mathcal{O}^{p}_{r,t}|}.
\label{eq:decayloss}
\end{equation}
The density is checkpoint prevalence of failure among observed,
previously acquired contracts. An empty observed denominator is undefined.
These summaries require known full-risk membership. If earlier acquisition
checkpoints leave membership uncertain, retain that uncertainty and the
checkpoint vectors rather than treating unobserved acquisition as failure
to acquire.
Publish the full risk set, observed subset and missing count
$M^p_{r,t}=|\mathcal{R}_{r,t}|-|\mathcal{O}^p_{r,t}|$ alongside it.
The full-risk loss count lies between $L^p_{r,t}$ and
$L^p_{r,t}+M^p_{r,t}$; missing evaluations cannot be treated as passes.
Never-acquired contracts remain acquisition failures.
Retain onset, recovery, recurrence and uncertainty across missing
checkpoints. Recovery does not erase an episode. Distinguish same-cycle
post-step losses and rejected attempts from losses in retained code.

The inspected v6 analyzer uses a stricter summary rule: incomplete prior
acquisition or any unobserved eligible result makes its loss count and
density unavailable, with completeness flags. Its standard export does
not emit the observed-subset counts, missing-count fields and bounds
specified above. Those are prospective reporting requirements. Before
collection, the author must authorize a source-bound implementation or
prospectively amend the reporting rule; this manuscript does neither.

\subsection{Replication and uncertainty}\label{sec:protocollimits}

The researcher prospectively selects $n\in\{12,21,33\}$ independent
repository trajectories per model per arm, with \textbf{12 as the default}.
Before study collection, record the choice, rationale, planning SD,
power/precision trade-off, date and source-bound configuration in the saved
study record and prospective protocol. Selecting a sample size does not
establish that its associated SD assumption is true.
The saved allocation is immutable on resume. Outcome-driven sample-size
changes, additions and replacements are prohibited; interim coverage,
loss, CR1 success or other outcomes cannot determine $n$.

The author selected a minimum useful absolute coverage gain of ten
percentage points, approximately thirteen test-weighted contracts.
Coverage scores $Y$ are expressed on the 0--100 scale. Let
$\overline{Y}_{ma}$ be mean final coverage for model $m$ and arm $a$.
The primary estimand is the equal-model-weight difference in mean coverage
over the $M=3$ selected models. Its estimator is
\begin{equation}
\widehat{\Delta}_{BD}=\frac{1}{M}
\sum_{m=1}^{M}(\overline{Y}_{mB}-\overline{Y}_{mD}).
\label{eq:primarycontrast}
\end{equation}
For balanced cells of $n_{\mathrm{cell}}$ independent trajectories and a
common within-cell variance $\sigma^2$, its planning variance is
$2\sigma^2/(M n_{\mathrm{cell}})$, with $n_{\mathrm{cell}}=n$.
For one two-sided comparison with $\alpha=0.05$, target power
$1-\beta=0.80$ and target difference $\Delta_*=10$ points, the normal
planning minimum, conditional on the assumed SD, is
\begin{equation}
n_{\mathrm{plan}}(\sigma)=
\left\lceil\frac{2(z_{1-\alpha/2}+z_{1-\beta})^2\sigma^2}
{M\Delta_*^2}\right\rceil.
\label{eq:samplesize}
\end{equation}

These are conditional planning assumptions, not estimates of actual
within-cell variability. Target power depends on independent trajectories,
the common variance, normal approximation and equal-model estimand; it is
not guaranteed by any offered choice. In particular, default $n=12$ is
\emph{not} an 80\%-power design under the existing SD of 25 points.
The bounded score's worst-case SD is 50, requiring 131 per cell under
the same approximation; that is outside the offered allocation choices.
The variance assumption and selected allocation require explicit author
review and prospective registration before collection.

The candidate independent-cell analysis resamples whole trajectories
within model and arm, with 10,000 draws and pointwise 95\% percentile
intervals. The inspected v6 code uses independent arm/model draws; it
does not preserve joint scheduling-block draws.
The inspected primary requires a complete allocated five-arm/model metadata
frame, known unassisted status and observed contributing B/D final coverage.
When that frame is unmet it reports the primary unavailable. Retain the
allocated frame and costs; secondary outputs cannot replace it with an
observed-model pooled or complete-case primary. An earlier missing H2
checkpoint can coexist with an observed final coverage or CR1 endpoint.
This availability rule also needs explicit adoption or prospective
revision in the approved study record.
Model-specific estimates and other contrasts are secondary, with no
family-wide coverage claim.

must reconcile this independent-trajectory variance and bootstrap scheme
with the model/replicate blocking. A scheduling label alone establishes
neither pairing nor independence. The author must justify scheduling-only
blocks with independent-trajectory inference, or separately approve a
block-aware analysis and aligned planning variance. Shared block/service
effects remain unmeasured; the v6 source freeze does not resolve this choice.

\subsection{Resources, feasibility and collection gates}\label{sec:prerun}

The actual aggregate envelope remains 300,000 AI credits, with a
270,000-credit local admission ceiling. Prior spending and provisions for
unknown historical usage remain charged or held against remaining
admission; unknown usage is never zero or free headroom.
Original 300-credit sessions use a 270-credit soft limit; D's
600-credit feature uses 540. A retry shares the original allowance.
In-flight responses can overshoot; these are not provider billing caps.
No verified dollar conversion is available.

Each study trajectory contains ten feature cycles and one CR1 session,
the latter capped at 45 minutes and 300 nominal credits in every arm.
A/D therefore have eleven sessions per trajectory; B/C/E have twenty-one.
A allows at most 5.75 agent-hours and 3,300 nominal credits per study
trajectory; B/C/D/E each allow 8.25 hours and 6,300 credits.
Across three models and five arms, the study has $15n$ trajectories,
$3n[2(11)+3(21)]=255n$ sessions,
$3n[5.75+4(8.25)]=116.25n$ maximum agent-hours and
$3n[3300+4(6300)]=85500n$ nominal session credits.

Excluded cost-only calibration retains one trajectory per model per arm:
15 trajectories, ten features each and no CR1. A/D have ten sessions and
B/C/E twenty; A allows five hours and 3,000 credits, and B/C/D/E each
allow 7.5 hours and 6,000 credits. Thus calibration adds 240 sessions,
105 maximum agent-hours and 81,000 nominal credits, independent of $n$.

Combined nominal allowances are $85500n+81000$ credits and maximum
agent-hours are $116.25n+105$. At default $n=12$, 1,107,000 nominal credits
and 3,300 sessions including calibration do not authorize spending beyond
the unchanged 300,000-credit envelope or 270,000-credit admission ceiling.
Excluded calibration informs cost and time feasibility only, not outcome-
or variance-driven sample resizing. Its cost forecast must show that the
entire selected allocation, including CR1 and all-attempt liabilities, fits
remaining admission and the deadline after prior spend and unknown-usage
holds. Otherwise halt before study collection. A different allocation
requires a separately authorized prospective amendment and a new saved
study, never a changed allocation on resume or a silent reduction to fit
the budget. An interrupted or unaffordable study retains its selected
allocation and is reported incomplete.
The inspected launcher enforces attempt and aggregate admission but does
not compute or approve that full-allocation forecast. A dated researcher
forecast and approval are required separately; a nonempty calibration
note is insufficient.
Proposed progression criteria are infrastructure failures at
most 5\%, no missing primary observations, complete billing/model/help
records, zero oracle contradictions in triplicate reference checks,
and no information-boundary failure. Hidden-test mutation assessment
must report its actual mutant denominator and unresolved equivalence.
These are feasibility criteria, inspired by pilot-study guidance
~\cite{eldridge2016pilot}, not a clinical CONSORT compliance claim.

Only current and previous visible specifications and examples reach
candidate tools. Hidden tests, scores, identifiers, messages, reference
code and future CR1 remain external. Candidate commands use no-network
containers; the trusted host SDK's provider connection is different.
The independent researcher receives a protected kit without access to
the original repositories or manuscript.
Use a researcher-managed workspace/account without the author's original
checkouts, manuscript or session-context caches; record actual access and
prior exposure rather than assuming blindness from a private flag.
The owner will make both
repositories private before collection and release approved materials
with the paper afterward. OSF registration/embargo and GitHub privacy
are separate; neither removes prior public or model exposure.
Fresh source-bound access checks, approval, freeze, prospective OSF
recording and launch permission remain required.

Unsuccessful feature increments are reset; later features and scheduled
B/C/E post-steps continue on retained green state. Hidden denominators
still include undelivered features. One infrastructure-only retry shares
the original budget; poor performance never justifies replay.
Contradictory valid oracle results halt collection.
Assisted and incomplete trajectories and all their costs are retained,
without replacements or success-conditioned primary analyses.
