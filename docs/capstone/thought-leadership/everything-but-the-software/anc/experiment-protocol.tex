\section{Testing the harness: an evolving exploratory experiment}\label{sec:experiment}

The experiment asks whether scheduled correction improves the repository
inherited by later feature work. RQ3 is addressed by a five-arm exploratory
study now in execution. Agent configuration protocols and empirical-study
guidance informed its earlier planning
~\cite{lulla2026protocol,baltes2026guidelines,wohlin2012experimentation}.

\subsection{Design and estimands}\label{sec:protocolsummary}

One independent replicate is a fresh repository trajectory through S1's
ten ordered features and a final held-out change request, CR1.
Every selected model, GPT~6.1 Sol, Sonnet~5.5 and Gemini~4 Argon,
runs every arm. Randomised interleaving within model/replicate blocks
uses seed 2027. Feature steps, tests and checkpoints are repeated
observations within a trajectory, not independent replicates; agent
sessions are not independent units either. The selected number $n$ applies
\emph{per model per arm}, equally across all fifteen cells.

\subsection{Outcomes and retained-contract loss}\label{sec:decaymeasurement}

The primary outcome is final behavioural coverage: contracts passed out
of all 127 scheduled feature contracts at $P_{10}$, before CR1.
Coverage combines acquisition, retention and repair.
Retained-contract loss and CR1 adaptation are distinct secondary outcomes,
with legacy identifiers H2 and H6 retained only for data traceability.
HIR/IDR definitions are in the supplementary appendices;
comprehension is not measured.

For trajectory $r$, $F_t$ is the retained state after feature step $t$ and
$P_t$ the state after its assigned post-step; these source states coincide
in A and D. Let $\mathcal{R}_{r,t}$ contain eligible, unchanged contracts
that passed at least one earlier end-of-cycle checkpoint $P_u$, $u<t$.
Superseded contracts leave this risk set. At $F_t$ and $P_t$, let
$\mathcal{O}^{p}_{r,t}\subseteq\mathcal{R}_{r,t}$ contain contracts with
observed binary results $S^p_{r,t}(q)$, $p\in\{F,P\}$. Report
\begin{equation}
L^p_{r,t}=\sum_{q\in\mathcal{O}^{p}_{r,t}}
\mathbf{1}\{S^p_{r,t}(q)=0\},\qquad
d^p_{r,t}=\frac{L^p_{r,t}}{|\mathcal{O}^{p}_{r,t}|}.
\label{eq:decayloss}
\end{equation}
The density is checkpoint prevalence of failure among observed,
previously acquired contracts. An empty observed denominator is undefined.
These summaries require known full-risk membership. If earlier acquisition
checkpoints leave membership uncertain, retain that uncertainty and the
checkpoint vectors rather than treating unobserved acquisition as failure
to acquire.
Publish the full risk set, observed subset and missing count
$M^p_{r,t}=|\mathcal{R}_{r,t}|-|\mathcal{O}^p_{r,t}|$ alongside it.
The full-risk loss count lies between $L^p_{r,t}$ and
$L^p_{r,t}+M^p_{r,t}$; missing evaluations cannot be treated as passes.
Never-acquired contracts remain acquisition failures.
Retain onset, recovery, recurrence and uncertainty across missing
checkpoints. Recovery does not erase an episode. Distinguish same-cycle
post-step losses and rejected attempts from losses in retained code.

The v6 analyzer inspected on 5 November uses a stricter summary rule: incomplete prior
acquisition or any unobserved eligible result makes its loss count and
density unavailable, with completeness flags. Its standard export does
not emit the observed-subset counts, missing-count fields and bounds
specified above. Those are prospective reporting requirements retained
from the earlier design. This update does not re-inspect the active
analyzer, establish that reporting pipeline or amend the reporting rule.
The source-bound reporting decision remains to be documented without
backdating an amendment or inferring completeness from raw vectors.

\subsection{Replication and uncertainty}\label{sec:protocollimits}

The 2 November planning record offers $n\in\{12,21,33\}$ independent
repository trajectories per model per arm, with \textbf{12 as the default};
current execution selects 21. That record calls for the choice, rationale,
planning SD, power/precision trade-off, date and source-bound configuration
to be recorded before collection. It does not establish that the current
amendments were prospectively registered. Selecting a sample size does not
establish that its associated SD assumption is true.
The saved allocation is immutable on resume. Outcome-driven sample-size
changes, additions and replacements are prohibited; interim coverage,
loss, CR1 success or other outcomes cannot determine $n$.

Servera Bondroit selected a minimum useful absolute coverage gain of ten
percentage points, approximately thirteen test-weighted contracts.
Coverage scores $Y$ are expressed on the 0--100 scale. Let
$\overline{Y}_{ma}$ be mean final coverage for model $m$ and arm $a$.
The primary estimand is the equal-model-weight difference in mean coverage
over the $M=3$ selected models. Its estimator is
\begin{equation}
\widehat{\Delta}_{BD}=\frac{1}{M}
\sum_{m=1}^{M}(\overline{Y}_{mB}-\overline{Y}_{mD}).
\label{eq:primarycontrast}
\end{equation}
For balanced cells of $n_{\mathrm{cell}}$ independent trajectories and a
common within-cell variance $\sigma^2$, its planning variance is
$2\sigma^2/(M n_{\mathrm{cell}})$, with $n_{\mathrm{cell}}=n$.
For one two-sided comparison with $\alpha=0.05$, target power
$1-\beta=0.80$ and target difference $\Delta_*=10$ points, the normal
planning minimum, conditional on the assumed SD, is
\begin{equation}
n_{\mathrm{plan}}(\sigma)=
\left\lceil\frac{2(z_{1-\alpha/2}+z_{1-\beta})^2\sigma^2}
{M\Delta_*^2}\right\rceil.
\label{eq:samplesize}
\end{equation}

The bounded score's worst-case SD is 50, requiring 131 per cell under
the same approximation; that is outside the offered allocation choices.
These planning requirements and assumptions are retained from the earlier
record. Neither the registered base nor selection of $n=21$ establishes
their validity for evolving-source exploratory data.

\subsection{Historical resource planning and operational boundaries}\label{sec:prerun}

The following resource scenarios and feasibility criteria retain the
2--5 November planning record. In particular, their full ten-feature
calibration differs from the short calibration disclosed for ongoing
unregistered execution. They are not measured costs, a current affordability
approval or evidence that historical launch gates were satisfied.

The actual aggregate envelope remains 300,000 AI credits, with a
270,000-credit local admission ceiling. Prior spending and provisions for
unknown historical usage remain charged or held against remaining
admission; unknown usage is never zero or free headroom.
Original 300-credit sessions use a 270-credit soft limit; D's
600-credit feature uses 540. A retry shares the original allowance.
In-flight responses can overshoot; these are not provider billing caps.
No verified dollar conversion is available.

Each study trajectory contains ten feature cycles and one CR1 session,
the latter capped at 45 minutes and 300 nominal credits in every arm.
A/D therefore have eleven sessions per trajectory; B/C/E have twenty-one.
A allows at most 5.75 agent-hours and 3,300 nominal credits per study
trajectory; B/C/D/E each allow 8.25 hours and 6,300 credits.
Across three models and five arms, the study has $15n$ trajectories,
$3n[2(11)+3(21)]=255n$ sessions,
$3n[5.75+4(8.25)]=116.25n$ maximum agent-hours and
$3n[3300+4(6300)]=85500n$ nominal session credits.

The historical excluded cost-only calibration scenario retains one trajectory per model per arm:
15 trajectories, ten features each and no CR1. A/D have ten sessions and
B/C/E twenty; A allows five hours and 3,000 credits, and B/C/D/E each
allow 7.5 hours and 6,000 credits. Thus calibration adds 240 sessions,
105 maximum agent-hours and 81,000 nominal credits, independent of $n$.
