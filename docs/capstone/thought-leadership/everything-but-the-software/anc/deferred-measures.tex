\subsection{Deferred intervention measures}\label{app:deferredmeasures}

Help and administrative records determine trajectory eligibility and cost,
but the study does not report intervention rates. The proposed measures
distinguish cycles receiving corrective help from cycles needing it. HG-DAgger and
ThriftyDAgger provide precedents for measuring intervention in other domains;
their findings do not establish effectiveness for coding agents
~\cite{kelly2019hgdagger,hoque2022thriftydagger}.

For run $r$, $n_r\geq1$ is attempted feature cycles, including assigned
post-steps and failed cycles, and $h_{rj}$ qualifying corrective-help
events. The full-run rates below require complete help annotation for every
attempted cycle. Otherwise report annotation coverage and leave the full-run
rate unknown. Observed human intervention rate would be
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{HIR}_r=\frac{1}{n_r}\sum_{j=1}^{n_r}
\mathbf{1}\{h_{rj}>0\}.
\label{eq:hir}
\end{equation}
HIR answers how often a cycle received corrective help, regardless of how
many events occurred or how long they took. Record event counts and active
human minutes separately. Let $d_{rj}\in\{0,1\}$ indicate an explicit
help request or a block needing corrective human action. The
intervention-demand rate (IDR) is the fraction of cycles requiring that help:
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{IDR}_r=\frac{1}{n_r}\sum_{j=1}^{n_r}d_{rj}.
\label{eq:intervention-demand}
\end{equation}
IDR counts cycles needing help, including requests that received none.
A timeout alone does not establish such a request; zero attempts gives
undefined rates. The experiment's eighteen GPT F9 sessions ending with
a request to extend the immutable \texttt{READ\_METHODS} test are
concrete demand candidates. Their recorded requests do not show that
anyone supplied the requested help; coding them would still require
cycle-level annotation and a complete denominator
(Section~\ref{app:executiondetail}).
A future annotation protocol would need to locate each event, cycle and
phase, check coverage and adjudicate unclear cases. Missing records leave
the rate unknown. Routine launches, planned approvals and safety stops are
not corrective rescue under these proposed definitions.

The original-case history provides qualitative steering examples: requests
for RPI tracking, migration, resource controls and functional checkpoints.
Within the retained coordinator session, 1,198 rows span the selected case
period. Of these, 1,066 user-message strings begin with the exact prefix
\texttt{[Scheduled prompt \#}; the other 132 rows have not been exhaustively
classified as manually authored corrective help. Repeated scheduled strings
occur among these records. The case evidence index retains the session,
date selection, turn locators and versioned launch prescriptions, together
with adverse assistant summaries of unresolved readiness and checkpoint
failures. Those summaries lack the underlying execution receipts.
Attempted-cycle denominators and complete help annotations remain
unavailable, so neither HIR nor IDR can be calculated. This case history is
separate from the completed experiment's owner-attested unassisted
trajectories.
