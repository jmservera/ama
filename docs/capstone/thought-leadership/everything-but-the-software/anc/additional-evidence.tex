\subsection{Additional source qualifications}\label{app:additionalevidence}

These qualifications apply to the literature discussed in
Section~\ref{sec:background}. The original-source check on 14 November
found that Laban et~al.'s arXiv v1 abstract and Section~6.1 report a 39\% relative
performance drop, whereas that version's Figure~1 caption says 35\%.
The main text uses the results passage's 39\%. The discrepancy within the
source remains unresolved, and neither value estimates repository decay.
Gloaguen et~al.'s Table~3 records the developer-versus-LLM comparison as
$p=0.038$, despite percentage-formatted p-values in the surrounding prose.
The manuscript uses the table value. Besker et~al.'s 43-developer/23\%
claim has institutional-abstract support; the full original and the
tool-generation qualifier in the Echoes of AI source remain subject to the
access limits recorded in the ancillary source review.

The provenance of merge-outcome studies also matters. Peralta et al.
start with 9,799 human-reviewed agent PRs and manually inspect 717.
Their 35.7\% clear-failure share among rejections and 15.4\% explicit
reviewer-involvement share among merges describe that manual sample
~\cite{peralta2026merged}. Murphy-Hill et al. estimate roughly 24\% more merges among adopters in an
observational rollout study~\cite{murphyhill2026agents}. That measures
output; the delivered value needs separate assessment. AIDev contains
932,791 agent-authored PRs from five agents across 116,211 repositories
~\cite{li2026aidev}. It provides a population for research without establishing
how many user outcomes were accepted.

Pavlenko et al.'s World Model offers an additional context-management
design: preserve raw evidence, derive rebuildable views and retrieve compact,
task-specific context with provenance and access controls
~\cite{pavlenko2026factory}. That architecture is an alternative to relying
on an expanding read path; its effect on the AMA case is untested.
